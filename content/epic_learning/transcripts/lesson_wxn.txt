In the previous module, we created and took advantage of LUTs to achieve a specific look and feel. The issue with that is how unreliable a LUT can be to ensure our colors are rendering properly on displays using a different screen space than ours. To adjust for this, we're going to color grade our scene using a post-process volume so that we can get the look we want and it is consistent on all the displays that may view it whether in low or high dynamic range. At a high level, the purpose of color grading or tone mapping our scenes is to both allow for the transformation of color spaces from HDR to LDR as well as color correction. We tone map in HDR because it gives us vastly more colors to work with and Unreal Engine will remap those to LDR for us. Unreal Engine's Filmic tone-mapper matches what's known as the Academy Color and Coding System or ASIS. This is the same tone-mapper used for TV and film and it ensures our colors are preserved across the many displays that will view our content.
