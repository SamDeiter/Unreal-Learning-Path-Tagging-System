Now that we have finished creating our example AI, let us compare the final result to the high level AI feedback loop introduced at the start of the course. The starting point of the course was to break down AI into three main categories, Sents, Think, and Act. We began with a navigation system, which could be considered a specialized kind of sense outside of the perception system. It allows the AI to send static obstacles as it creates a path to its target location. It can also use this to determine if it's possible to occupy a certain space without having to bump into something first. Naturally, everything covered by the AI perception system can be placed within the sense category. This would also cover any additional senses that we would add via the perception system. Everything contained within our behaviour tree and blackboard covered the Think category. This category takes all the sense information, such as a valid path to a location or a reference to a currently perceived actor, and makes a decision based upon that information. This brings us to Act. The Act category covers anything we actually got our AI to do within the level. In our example, everything that happened was part of a task from the behaviour tree. Our examples focused on movement, but it could just as easily have been playing an animation, making a sound, or sending a message to another AI. This loops back around neatly to the senses. When a task on the behaviour tree is completed, we finish a sequence, which then leads to our decorators being re-evaluated. It's worth noting that due to the nature of behaviour trees and the perception system itself, this event-based system can fire off independently rather than running in a rigid loop. However, as we plan our high-level task flow, this is a useful method for conceptualising the AI process. In this video, we saw how the various AI systems we have used correlate to the high-level concepts introduced at the beginning of the course. We saw how the AI perception, EQS and navigation systems could be considered as being within the sense category, and how the behaviour tree assets could be considered the think category. Finally, the tasks within the behaviour tree branches could be considered the Act category.
