Nanite is virtualized geometry. It allows you to create projects with massive amounts of geometric detail. You can directly import film quality source art composed of millions of polygons, anything from zebra sculpts to photogrammetry scans, and place them millions of times all while maintaining a real-time frame rate and without any noticeable loss of fidelity. Nanite intelligently streams and processes only the detail that you can perceive, largely removing polycount and draw-call constraints. It eliminates time-consuming work like baking details to normal maps and manually authoring LODs, framed you up to concentrate on creativity. So how is it Nanite mesh different from a stack mesh? A Nanite mesh, which we can see here on the right, is essentially a triangle mesh at its core, with a lot of level of detail and compression applied to its data. On top of that, Nanite uses an entirely new system for rendering that data format in an extremely efficient way. Adding content for Nanite is no different than traditional meshes, except that Nanite can handle orders of magnitude more triangles and instances. Move the camera close enough and Nanite will draw the original source triangles that were imported. Nanite meshes also support multiple UVs and vertex colors. Materials are assigned to the sections of the mesh that can be done in shaders. Material assignments can be swapped dynamically just like any other stack mesh, and Nanite doesn't require any process to bake down materials. The primary differences between a Nanite mesh and a stack mesh is that any stack mesh with Nanite enabled will typically render faster and take less memory, and materials that are used to simulate high density objects such as fences can now just be modeled as is. So how does Nanite work? During import, meshes are analyzed and broken down into high-archial clusters of triangle groups. During rendering, clusters are swapped on the fly at varying levels of detail, based on the camera view, and connect perfectly without cracks to neighboring clusters within the same object. Data is streamed in on-demand so that only visible details need to reside in memory. Nanite runs in its own rendering pass that completely bypasses traditional draw calls. Visualization modes can be used to inspect the Nanite pipeline. Lastly, when working with Nanite, data size is one of the benefits. We have both the size on disk as well as the size in memory. If we were to look at our normal stack mesh and go to the size map, we can see it takes up approximately 408 megabytes on disk. With the majority of it being the stack mesh and the rest being the materials. If we look at our memory size, we can see it takes up about 440 megabytes in memory when it loads up this asset. Now if we compare this to our unoptimized Nanite mesh in the size map, we can see it takes up approximately the same size on disk, that's because we haven't optimized the texture usage. But our memory size is significantly smaller at only 4.4 megabytes. This is one of the advantages of Nanite as it only uses the data it needs and it has a high compression. Now we're free to open up our meshes and look at them in more detail. We can see our original stack mesh is around 4 million triangles and is estimated to take around 190 megabytes of compressed disk size. There are no level of details, so this is the worst case scenario where we have 4 million triangles per mesh displayed on our screen. Looking at the Nanite mesh, we can see we still have approximately 4 million Nanite triangles. However, based on our display, it's going to show less and we can see it only takes up 40 megabytes of compressed disk size. Looking at the wireframe mode on our stack mesh, we can see as we have no LODs, we have a large amount of triangles being displayed, even if we are at a far away distance. Comparing this to our Nanite mesh, it will adjust the amount of triangles on our screen as the item gets farther away from our camera for more performance and we get this benefit without having to pre-bake out level of detail. Nanite is a powerful tool that allows us to add near infinite detail to our projects.
