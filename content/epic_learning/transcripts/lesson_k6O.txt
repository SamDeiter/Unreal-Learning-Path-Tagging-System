Stage framing and experience for your end users that may be used on devices other than the HoloLens or even across larger than 5-meter spaces is important to ensure that regardless of device, each person has the same experience. In this module, we will discuss stage framing for persistent holograms. Designing content that is intended to work on multiple devices requires consideration as to where the camera is located in the world, as well as what it needs to keep track of. There are multiple frames of references we can use to best accomplish the design goals of our projects. All of the maps we have worked through so far have been small, room scale experiences constrained to a 5-meter space. This has allowed the stationary frame of reference that HoloLens 2 uses to focus on keeping the holograms as stable as possible despite us turning our head or walking around. Both the HoloLens and Windows Mixed Reality VR devices will use this stationary frame of reference. This method of managing the coordinate systems around us will put heavy focus on the scanned environment, so our project will behave the same whether it is viewed through a HoloLens or a capable VR device. There are projects that require the user despite the device used and the user's height to start at the same location. This could be a seated scale experience where the user is placed in the driver's seat of a car. We would of course want every user to be able to access the controls. Another example is what we have been working on in these courses. In this project, the only hologram that contains an important location is the architecture covering up my bookshelf. I would like any other person that wanted to experience this project here to put on a capable device and the bookcase cover to be in the same spot. Of course, there are other types of projects that require or allow the user's device or height to alter the experience for them. If that project was intended to play on a VR device as well as on the HoloLens, we need to prepare our project to alter the way it handles the device used. And regardless of which frame of reference we are wanting to use, the larger scale experiences will require a more dedicated use of spatial anchors to maintain accuracy. Beyond the 5 meter room scale experiences, our devices can inadvertently become unreliable with keeping holograms accurate to their dedicated location if we rely on the single coordinate system. If, for example, we have a large building that we want to provide wave finding services to guests, we can have the spatial anchors distributed around the floors or the walls or ceilings or simply floating around. Expanding from the single rigid coordinate system is needed to maintain accuracy with these large scales. We will need an alternative to the single global coordinate system we have been using. Luckily, what is happening behind the scenes is being taken care of by the HoloLens. It is constantly sensing the world around us over time while we use the project and creating an understanding of the coordinates required to maintain these locational spatial anchors around our worlds. Using the spatial anchoring system will place substantial focus on the areas we want to specifically have the ability to pin our holograms to. Sharing the anchors we place around our scene will have the same expectation of accurate location regardless of the device used. So while our HoloLens will use our eye level as the tracking origin by default, some devices will use the floor for its origin. If your project has these requirements, the nodes you will want to use are the Git tracking origin node and the set tracking origin node. If you want to force the HoloLens frame of reference from eye level to floor level, check if the tracking origin is defaulting to eye level and if so, add local offset in the Z to the value of the player's height. This will also require that your camera component is placed directly on the floor of your map. If instead, the need was to change a floor tracking origin based device, we can check if the tracking origin is floor level and if so, set it to eye level. The goal with this change is to conform all the devices used to take advantage of the same system for consistency. We then learned how to alter a device's default frame of reference to maintain consistency for multi-device project usage.
