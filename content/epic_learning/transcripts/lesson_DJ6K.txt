Before Rocky takes you on a journey of how to engage in more respectful design to create a more inclusive and exciting virtual world, it's important to consider why appearance diversity is important in games, especially when it comes to women and girls. Around the world, 1.3 billion women play video games. Yet despite the fact that women comprise almost half of the global games community, new research from Dubb shows that 73% of players who identify as women believe that there is a lack of diversity in the world of games. This presents us with an exciting opportunity to explore how we can use creativity, innovation and self-expression to portray beauty and appearance in the virtual world through more inclusive and diverse design. But first, let's consider what game environments currently look like for women and girls. My team recently took a deep dive into over 50 scientific studies published over the past decade. Encouragingly, we found that from the 1980s to the 2000s, positive depictions of female characters and the number of playable female characters have increased. Even so, the percentage of primary female characters has not grown. They remain underrepresented when compared to male and non-human characters. Moreover, female characters often do not represent the amazing diversity of beauty and appearance we see in everyday life. So, as it stands, we're not fully capitalising on all of the possibilities for creativity, diversity and self-expression that the virtual world can offer us. This training is designed to help you unlock this opportunity through respectful character design. Beyond appearance, we also have a big opportunity in the virtual world when it comes to creating female characters in positions of power, with agency and being valued for attributes beyond their appearance. Historically, the most common tropes within hardcore games for female characters include the damsel and distress and being featured as background decorations or rewards. So, not only have the appearances of female characters being limited, but their personas, storylines and performances have also been limited by gender stereotypes. As a result, we have not been fully capturing the richness of women's capabilities and strengths in the virtual world. As we move into the world of meta-humans, it's a good opportunity to reflect on how we can create a more inclusive and diverse virtual world when it comes to the representation of female characters. So, you may be thinking, why is it worth investing your time and energy into the real virtual beauty training and creating more diverse and authentic characters? Doves research found 60% of girls feel that the current lack of diversity in female characters in games has a negative effect on their self-esteem. This is backed up by rigorous scientific studies that have shown that playing games and in particular those that have a lack of diversity in female characters can be associated with negative outcomes for gamers. This includes low-body confidence and a tendency to internalise gender stereotypes like the idea that women should be caregivers and pursue traditional feminine careers, avoiding things like science, technology and maths. For example, Slater and colleagues published an experiment in 2017, which found that when girls play appearance-focused games, they're more likely to experience low-body confidence and a preference for stereotypical female careers as compared to girls who played with a non-appearance-focused game. Similarly, another study by Basabas and Sibli published in 2020 found that the more women in men play games, the more likely they were to experience body dissatisfaction and low self-esteem. Self-esteem, body confidence and being able to freely express yourself are important and can affect key areas of people's lives, including their mental and physical health, their social relationships and their aspirations at school and work. By diversifying the appearance and functionality of your characters, particularly your female characters, you can be part of a big movement to improve the well-being of players and the shared spaces they enjoy together. In short, a diverse and inclusive approach to appearance and beauty matters, and by designing more diverse and authentic characters, you're driving positive social change.
