Lens flare is an image-based technique that simulates the scattering of light when viewing bright objects due to imperfections and camera lenses. In a camera lens, lens flares occur when light comes into the front of the lens at a narrow angle and it reflects between different surfaces of the lens elements. Chromatic aberration, or scene fringe, is an effect that simulates the color shift in real world camera lenses. This is most noticeable near the edge of the image due to the curvature of the lens. In real world cameras, you can sometimes notice this when a dark object is in front of a brighter background. Chromatic aberration can add a cool, techy sci-fi sort of look, but I tend to avoid it for traditional architectural visualization. Color grading is the general process of improving the appearance of an image by adjusting attributes such as contrast, color, and saturation. In our Post Process material, we have a ton of control over the attributes of shadows, highlights, and mid-tone values.
