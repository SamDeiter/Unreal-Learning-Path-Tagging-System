However, don't worry about the math and the actual nodes. I will try to explain the entire logic of materials from the absolute basics. So before we jump into practice, let's start with basic theory. Let me explain how a pair of numbers between 0 and 1 can represent a position on a texture. Since the dawn of digital media, the concept of pixels has existed as the core of image editing. A bitmap is an array of pixels in two dimensions, X and Y. X being the horizontal, Y being the vertical. It was therefore natural to provide a position on a bitmap as a pair of integer numbers, that is, coordinates in pixels. The coming of 3D graphic accelerators brought a new concept to the front, texture coordinates. They are expected to be a pair of floating point numbers. The space between the four corners of an image, the texture, is called the UV space. UV in that name are just two symbols, nothing more. They are the same as X and Y, just differentiated so we don't confuse them with screen coordinates. Unlike positions in pixels, the UVs begin at 0 on one edge and 1 on the opposite edge. The center of the image lies at half. And that is true for every image, no matter its resolution or dimensions. The 0 to 1 range idea brings many benefits. First, the UVs are continuous. So as you can see, there is no division into single pixels. In the context of the texture being sampled, it makes it look smooth compared to the actual low resolution bitmap. The values in between the actual text cells are interpolated. And by default, this is so-called linear interpolation. The other benefit is that the UVs are universal. They are independent from the texture size in pixels. So it's always between 0 and 1. In this example, you can see how stretching the widget keeps the UVs unchanged. Still, every corner is represented by the same value, here visualized as color. So since we are on the topic of visualization, the UVs are just a set of numbers. So they are a vector in the computer graphics parlance, meaning they can be used to represent a position in space. But colors are also vectors. So coordinates like UVW or XYZ can be almost interchangeable with R, G and B, so the components of a color. Therefore, if I tell the graphics card to just display my U and V as red and green, this is what I get. I get each position visualized as color, as pair of red and green intensity. Of course, 1 means maximum green or maximum red and blacks mean 0. Bottom right corner is yellow because it's both max green and red at the same time. So you can imagine these corners as 0, 0, then 1 and 0, bottom left is 0 and 1, and bottom right is 1 and 1.
