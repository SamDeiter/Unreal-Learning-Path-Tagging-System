Welcome to the introduction to using a post-processing volume in Unreal Engine 5. My name is Kevin Lyall, and my team and I have put this course together to give you a tour of the tools available to you that you can use to take artistic control of your scenes. We're going to jump right into the new ArchViz template that is included with Unreal Engine 5 and focus on creating cinematic and stylistic looks using post-processing volumes. Feel free to experiment on your own project or start a new project in Unreal Engine 5 and choose the architectural visualization template to follow along. If you are interested in getting your hands on what is created or used here, all the files will be available to you. That includes the fantastic set of post-processing materials provided by our own Tom Shannon. This course is written for someone who is either new to Unreal Engine altogether but has a good idea of how to move around the interface and wants to focus on rendering. It is also written for someone who simply wants to learn some new techniques to controlling the overall look of their scenes. We'll be covering a range of topics such as what a post-processing volume is to some more intermediate concepts like how to color grade your scenes using best practices to get you started. Following this intro to the course, we're going to discuss what a post-processing volume is and what kinds of things we can achieve by using them in our scenes. Then we will go through the project settings to prepare a neutralized scene to give us a baseline to build up from. We'll experiment with using multiple post-processing volumes in one scene and then move on to creating both stylistic and cinematic effects respectively. We'll then move on to quickly color grading our scene using a custom let. After that, we will match the look by using our post-processing volume to give us a proper color grade with an updated workflow. We'll get to take advantage of the new Lumen system in this course and there are some key points to point out with that change. Finally, we'll end with the discussion of additional use cases and where to go from here. We have lots to get to. Let's get started in the next module by identifying what a post-processing volume is, where to find it in the editor and how to use it.
