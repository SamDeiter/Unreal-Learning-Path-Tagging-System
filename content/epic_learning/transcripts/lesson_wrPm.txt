One important aspect of design is to be aware of metrics. Metrics is a scale or dimensions of the pieces of your scene that are determined by design as there are specific values that influence how the game is played or how interactions with players work. In this image, we can see our example scale figures next to the windows and doors of the scene. Typically, these are sometimes most important sizes to be aware of when it comes to metrics. Besides the door may be exaggerated to allow easy flow, the character to come in and out, and the window may be sized to allow the character to vault over the window frame to get in and out of the building quickly during gameplay. Many times the studio will have design documents that denote the specific sizes they keep for doors, windows, stairs, railing, thickness of walls and floors, heights a player can jump or fall from, and more to ensure that the consistency is maintained throughout the project. When design works on a level or scene, this is one of the main aspects they focus on while designing the space for the block out. Another key aspect to keep in mind as an artist replacing the block out meshes is to pay close attention to collisions. Collisions are how a player collides or interacts with the meshes and assets in the space. While a block out may seem fairly simple when you get it, you will want to maintain that anything you're adding is not blocking the player in a way that makes traversing the space more difficult or frustrating. Collisions like stairs often have a specific size the steps must be to allow the player to smooth each reverse them, making them too big and the player might get caught up or get stopped while trying to walk up the stairs, making for a poor gameplay experience in the midst of a gameplay action. Collisions and other assets you add to the scene like chairs and tables are also very important. If you move a chair too close to a window and there's a player trying to jump out or vault over the window, it may block the interaction or transition from happening correctly and smoothly. Be aware that asset placement at all times throughout the space as it's always important to best check with design if you're concerned that an asset may be providing a difficult gameplay experience. Collisions are also important when it comes to smaller props. Typically things like grass, small rocks, props on the tables and things that are smaller in size don't receive full collisions as they may obstruct the player, but they might receive special collisions that block bullets or gunfire or other interactions. Another commonly overlooked design aspect for artists that can be a challenge is the line of sight. Often times in games we're seeing another player from a distance is important. There are some key decisions that are made by design as to how to allow or disallow for visibility at certain points throughout the level. Keeping a close eye on working with design to ensure sight lines are maintained where they're important can be a critical part of gameplay. As an example, something like a tree may completely block the line of sight for a player across the map, potentially making a player class like a sniper less useful or beneficial for the player to choose or pick, especially if it's used so often that it makes it never viable for them to use their long range ability. The same can be said about color choices as well when it comes to art and line of sight. Often times games will avoid making the building or setting the same color or tone as the player character to allow players to be more easily seen at a distance.
