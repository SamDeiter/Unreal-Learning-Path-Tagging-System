Now to create this effect, we are going to be using volumetric light maps. And so, if we come to the show button on our 3D view, and then to the visualized fly-out, we can turn on the volumetric light map option. These are essentially points in space in the level that have both captured and stored lighting information for that particular set of coordinates. Now these can be used in Unreal in a number of ways, such as to help light moving objects with global illumination, or as in our case, help build volumetric fog effects. Now, at this moment in time, though, you can see that we have very few volumetric samples in the scene. And so, let's help unreal out a little by coming to the world settings tab, finding the light mass rollout, and then decrease the cell size parameter, which will in turn increase the resolution of the samples in the scene. Now I'm being a bit drastic here so that we can clearly see the difference this will make, but do bear in mind that this will also alter light build times by quite a bit. A value of 50 here will work nicely for me. Now we may also need to increase the brick memory value when we decrease the cell size by quite a bit, as we don't want Unreal running out of memory when we bake the lighting solution. And now, though, I will leave this as is, and re-bake our lighting in order to see the change that this makes. Which as you can see, greatly increases the number of samples in our scene. Coming back to the show menu, in the 3D view, let's click to turn off the volumetric samples visualiser, and then, coming to the modes tab, let's search far and add an exponential height fog to our scene. This adds a volume fog to the scene that acts and behaves in a much more realistic way than other legacy options. Of course, it is a little more taxing when it comes to computation time, but for visualization pieces such as this, we should be fine. Coming to its parameters, let's set the fog density value to 0.05, enable the volumetric fog checkbox, and then change how much of an effect this is having on the scene by setting the extinction scale to a value of 10. This just sets how much the geometry in the scene will block light passing through the fog, thus making effects such as light shafts more visible, and as we move our camera around, we can see that we are indeed producing a nice effect.
