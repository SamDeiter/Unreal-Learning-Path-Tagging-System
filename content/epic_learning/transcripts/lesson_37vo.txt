Interacting with your project and solving problems that are dynamically presented to you, regardless of situation, requires on-the-fly thinking and a set of tools that need to be at the ready. In this module, let's discuss some reasons why we would want to design mechanics that don't rely on us using our hands. It is not difficult to imagine situations where a person's hands are both in use yet are required to do an additional task. The desire for more hands to assist them is an age-old one, and now we have devices to create these additional means of interaction. From designing important mechanics to add to the accessibility of your project, to increasing the means that an end user can operate a device from a distance while productive with another task. Adding the ability to take advantage of two new deltas in your interactable toolkit will add another very natural extension to the experience. The HoloLens 2 is fully capable of both head and eye tracking. We can retrieve this data and use it in our projects for many purposes. Let's begin with ease of use. Just as we naturally move a mouse around on a 2D screen to solve problems, so 2 can use our eyes and head to solve similar problems in 3D. Interacting with a far-off menu, selecting a tool to use, navigating and instruction, identifying points or zones of interest, creating or setting up parameters in your scene, are just a few examples of how you might complement traditional mechanics with assistance from your gaze. Creating the interaction models in each of your projects must be based on the initial goals set out for it. If, for example, the project is to instruct a user through a task, they may need to use their hands to achieve it. While their hands are busy, it certainly would make things easier if they had a way to interact with the instruction using their eyes. We can use the retrieved data to determine where they are looking and, equally important, where they are not looking. This will become important for providing them relevant information when and where they need it. Another benefit for hands for interaction is to reduce the chances of causing fatigue in our end users. It's not a great idea to request them to keep their arms and hands up and working and interacting while expecting precision with the augmented scene. And that's without the assumption of them needing to hold heavy tools or operate delicate machines. The project we create should enable a continuous experience to assist them with their goals and never be a hindrance. Speaking of tools and machinery, the HoloLens 2 is built to track a user's bare hands. If they wear gloves for protection or are carrying a tool, the tracking will become unreliable, enabling the switching to hands-free interaction will be the obvious fix, but the project needs to be ready for this. The next reason we'll discuss has to do with space. Both too much and too little can each provide their own issues. Too small a space will prevent the ability to move one's arms around to interact with the scene. If the space is too tight, it would be extremely advantageous to have a tool they could operate without needing to reach out with their hands or can tort their body to see the instruction at a better angle. If the project goals require it, they should be able to fully operate the experience without extra, unneeded or unsafe movements. The other side of this issue is with too much space. Imagine needing to repair several objects in a large room and needing to walk back to where the app was opened to change a setting or review the instruction. Creating a way for a hologram to follow the user around wherever they go will assist with this and prevent them from needing to walk to the hologram. So another important reason hands-free interaction is needed that we'll discuss is perhaps a silly one. I'm not saying it's the V reason you aren't seeing me on camera and are only seeing my screen or point of view, but I'm really glad that you're not. It can be socially awkward to move your arms around to seemingly random points and gesturing to interact when others can see you. It adds to the silliness if they are not in the same experience where they cannot view what is happening. Giving the option to operate the experience without the need for introducing potentially embarrassing moments, especially if not required for the project, would most likely be a welcome mechanic. In this module, we discuss some reasons why hand-free interaction matters, from a point of ease and safety to avoiding a potentially awkward situation.
