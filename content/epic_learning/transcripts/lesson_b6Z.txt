So what exactly is a master material? A master material is the ultimate parent material if you're seeing. The one that every child material instance in your world references and uses to determine what functionality it's going to have. So for example, let's say you have a new rock mesh that you're making your material for. So normally you just create a new material, plug in your textures, and you before done. Right? Now let's say you have to make five rocks, house some barrels, and each one needs snow to procedurally spawn on top of it. So you could manually make 50 unique materials that will procedurally blend snow, or you could make a master material and use it to create what's called material instances, children of the master material for each different material in your scene. So if you were to add the function of procedural snow into the master material, you'd also be adding it as an option into every child material instance in your world that references that master material. So it's the most time saving optimized way of creating your materials. And if any issues happen to crop up in your materials, like say they're too expensive, they have broken functionality, they need global tweaks, whatever it may be, all you have to do to fix them across every mesh in your world is fix them once in that one master material.
