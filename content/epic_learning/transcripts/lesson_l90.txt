Having already noted how the size of our landscapes can affect the overall performance of a visualization level, what we want to do is point you in the direction of a quick guide to both landscape size and a clearer understanding of how that can affect level performance. That way, even if we should forget the points that are being covered here, we will still have a nice simple way to jug our memory. Starting up our web browser of choice then, let's navigate to the docs.unreal.com website. Then once there, we can come to the search bar, found at the top, and type the words landscape sizes. From the filtered list that appears, we will want to click on the landscape technical guide entry, simply because the info found here can give us a really good starting point from which to work when creating our landscapes. Now I have opted to dedicate a video to just showing you where this guide is, as opposed to just mentioning it, because most people tend to either just skip over this or never go and take a look at it at all, which is a real shame given that reading through and then digesting the info found here can really help us make sure that our projects run smoothly and that our terrains perform well. A good idea then is to simply start a new Unreal project for ourselves and then in the terrain creation controls that we have already looked at, simply enter the values being shown here and make a note of how each one reacts as the terrain is sculpted. It would also be good to make note of how large the terrain is in the scene in relation to a static mesh that we already know the scale of. This can really help us get a good idea per project as to which starting point would perhaps be best for us. Knowing where this landscape quick guide can be found, can certainly save us time and computing resources if we use it as a go-to reference regarding the impact that the terrain can have on a project.
