In this video, we'll quickly review what we have created during the course to make a more advanced Paper2D project. Let's quickly review everything that we built during this course. We began by modifying the existing Paper2D material and rendering settings to support dynamic lighting. We then came to understand the limitations of the project's current camera projection type and rendering mode. With this in mind, we modified the camera projection type and added player control over the camera position. Once that was done, we added an enemy character complete with new textures and flipbook animations. We then added an AI controller and prefigured the enemy to move as well as avoid walls and platform edges. With that in place, we used Unreal Engine's damage system to send and receive damage between the player and enemy when they overlap with each other. To make things a bit more interesting, we added additional logic to detect when the player is landing on top of an enemy and only cause damage to them when this occurs. With all these elements in place, we finished the project with a strong foundation to build out a more complex 2D game. In this short video, we reviewed all the elements we have built together during this project and how they came together to build a solid foundation for a fully fledged 2D game experience.
