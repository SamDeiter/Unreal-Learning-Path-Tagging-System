Now, the next feature I want to talk about is actually really tricky for me to demonstrate in the Editor because it is free and it takes place entirely under the hood. I get to talk with my hands for a minute. If you have a Dynamic Light that is not moving, say on a light post, and you have a Dynamic, which is also not moving like a building, why would you constantly cast shadows onto that Dynamic Object? It gets expensive after a while. We all know Dynamic Shadows are very pricey on a GPU. What we do instead is we cache those shadows. The very first time we cast a shadow between two Dynamic Objects, we store that, and then as long as neither the light nor the object move, we just use the cache, and we do not recalculate over and over again. In a sample scene where we have three shadow casting lights, performance goes from 15 milliseconds for just those three lights down to less than one. You should note though that, well, first off, this is free. You do not have to set anything up. It just automatically works as long as your Dynamic Light and the Dynamic Object it is affecting are not moving. Also, you are going to incur full cost on that first shadow cast while you cache that up. At that point, as long as nothing moves, you are fine. Until something changes, then it writes another cache.
