Deploying from Windows to Android is a slightly trickier process than packaging directly to Windows. We will need some special connectivity tools to make sure your Android phone or tablet and UE4 work well together. Lucky for us, UE4 has made this process as easy as possible. First, let's navigate to our UE4 Android Works installation folder. This folder will be located by default at the C drive, then program files, then Epic Games, then UE4 point whatever version you're running, then Engine, then Extras, then Android Works, and lastly the Win64 folder. Now simply click Code Works for Android and it will take a moment to launch. Give it permissions to run and follow the instructions in the installer and choose Install. Once complete, you should now be looking at the Code Works Manager window. Simply select Next on the bottom right and choose Accept All and Accept to complete the installation. This installation will take some time to complete and requires a restart. You can see the progress in the progress column. Now that Code Works is installed, we need to set up our phone to ensure it is able to connect to our computer. I am using a Google Pixel 3 on Android 9. There will be subtle differences from phone to phone and version to version, but for the most part they will all follow a very similar Norman creature. Now let's connect our phone via a data capable USB cable. Most devices will connect automatically. In the off chance you need to find a USB driver to connect to your phone, you can check here. It is developer.android.com slash tools slash extras slash OEM-USB.html. If you hear the USB connection sound and do not get any error messages, it's probably working. Now the next step will be to unlock your device. On your connected Android device, navigate to your settings panel. Next, scroll down to about phone and select it. Next, find the build number. And on my phone, developer options are already unlocked. However, you just need to tap the build about 10 times to unlock it. Once you tap it a few times, it will notify you how many more times you need to tap. Once unlocked, it lets you know you are in developer mode. Back out and go to system, advanced, developer options and enable USB debugging. Lastly, let's jump back to the settings main page and jump to connected devices and enable file transfer. We can now put down our phones and jump back to the computer and test to be sure that the phone is all set. Our last step in our Android setup is to simply check and see if our phone is working correctly. Click the start button and type command prompt and then select it. In the command prompt window type ADB Devices and your device should be listed here. We are all set. Your Android phone is now ready to have UE4 connect to and work with your device.
