Hello there and welcome to our terrain essential concepts course, where we will be looking to get to grips with the tools and options found in Unreal for the creation of landscapes that can then be used inside our visualization projects. Now to help with that we will work at creating landscapes from image files, from components and sections. We will look at using these sculpting brushes to our advantage including noise, erosion and ramp brushes. And we will also cover the setup of materials that can be painted on our terrain surface for a more realistic look. To go that step further, we will also cover adding foliage to that landscape and then finally discuss some important concepts based around collisions and the debugging of our terrain. Now this course has mostly been designed for people who have never used the terrain building tools inside of Unreal before, but who would now like to see how they might introduce landscapes into their visualization projects. Even if you have zero knowledge of the tools available for landscapes then you will still be able to follow along with this course and benefit from the material cover. That being said though, the course hasn't been designed for people who are completely new to Unreal itself. And so, even though I will be explaining where we are going and what we are doing inside the UI as we move forward, I won't be covering things like basic usage of the editor. And so if you feel that this could perhaps hinder your learning progress then please feel free to watch some of the courses already found in the Academy library that deal with basic engine usage first of all. After which, you can then come back to this course and enjoy the full learning experience that it offers. The provided class files download for this course consists of just a single Unreal project that can be saved to disk and then opened either via the Epic launcher or by simply clicking on the .u project file that is located in the exercise files folder structure. Inside of which we have a levels folder that contains both start and final level versions. Although every size of effort has been made to ensure that both of these are conflict free, we may find as we work through the course that we need to use different names on some of the assets if they already exist.
