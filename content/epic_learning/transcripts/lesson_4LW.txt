As we demonstrated in this course, post-processing volumes can be quickly set up to provide global and or local effects, but they can also be used to achieve specific effects that last for short bursts or are very locational. There are many ways to take advantage of them that we did not cover here. Some examples are to provide feedback for a user interaction or to update them on something that happened in the map or rather didn't happen. Perhaps the character has entered a room that affects their vision. We could use a post-process volume specifically in that room to render that effect, or perhaps the heat has turned way up in a section of the map and you want to show heat waves gliding across the display. You could create that material effect and apply it to a post-process volume and control the intensity of it using its blend weight. Utilizing post-process volumes is a quick and optimized way of achieving the look and feel our projects require, but they are not free. Be sure to keep an eye out for how much additional resources are needed to render these additional effects.
