Although we have at this point left painting behind, there will be times in the later stages of a project where we might need a better idea of how our already painted layers of working are being applied to a terrain. This could be either for the purpose of adding further improvements or maybe for just trying to spot any problem areas that may exist. Which is why I have included the tool that we are about to look at here, given that it is used for debugging rather than painting purposes. Let's take a look at them how we can very quickly visualize just where our painted layers are on a terrain and how they are blending together. First of all then, we want to make sure that we have opened our ongoing project and that we actually have a painted terrain sitting there. Next, we want to make sure that we are inside the paint tab, as when we apply our viewport visualiser options, this is where they will appear so as to help us debug our painting. And of course it also means that we are ready should we decide that we need to do so to actually make some changes. To see how well our in our case how rushed our layer painting has been, we can come to the view mode drop down and then hover our mouse over the visualiser section so as to gain access to its options. After which, from the list that appears, we want to select the in layer debug option. Which will add a few extra checkboxes under each of the layers found in the modes tab. Let's put a check in the airbox, further grass layer and then put a check in the G box further rock layer. What we get now of course is a much clearer idea of just where our painted layers are and how they are blending together. And although it isn't recommended that we paint whilst in this mode, it does give us a very clear view of where we could perhaps improve both placement and our blending of our paint layers. Knowing how we can quickly inspect painted layers on a terrain can of course be a massive help when it comes to improving the overall quality of the landscapes that we are producing.
