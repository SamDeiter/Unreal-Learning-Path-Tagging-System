Hello and welcome to the ambient and procedural sound design course. I'll be doing the talking in these videos but the course has been put together by myself and my colleague Dave Raybold. You can follow Dave on Twitter at the new Tide. We've been working together for a long time and we've written two books on Unreal Engine Audio, the now legendary Game Audio tutorial and the more recent Game Audio implementation. We also write papers and book chapters on Game Audio and we teach the sound and music for interactive games Master's Course at Leeds Beckett University. We keep an up to date list of useful books and reading around the subject of video game audio at our website GameAudio Implementation.com. In this course, we'll be taking you through the basics of implementing audio into your Unreal Engine game. We'll be focused on creating the ambient sound for your level. That is the audio backdrop against which everything else sits but we'll also be looking at some important basic principles of interactive audio design introducing you to sound cues and getting you started with using blue prints for audio. We're going to assume that you're familiar with an audio editor like Reaper or Pro Tools so you can edit sounds or music ready to bring into the engine. We'll take you through things step by step but some basic understanding of the engine will help you so we'd recommend watching the following courses first. The Introducing Unreal Engine Course, the Unreal Editor Fundamentals Introduction Course and the Blue Prince Essential Concepts. Throughout the course we'll be building up the audio in a demonstration game project. You'll be able to download the final complete version and will be including an empty version with no audio in it at all so that you can populate it with your own audio and audio systems, trying out the techniques yourself as we go along. Each video covers a specific topic but some rely on knowledge from previous videos so if you know what you're interested in finding out about you can just jump straight to that video but if you're new to Unreal Engine Audio and it's probably best to follow them along from the start. In this course you'll be learning how to input audio into the engine and some tips for keeping your project and files organized. You'll learn how to add sounds to your game level focusing initially on loops but then quickly moving on to look at how one shot can really bring the world to life. We'll talk about different approaches to spatialising your sounds, how to use attenuation settings and the concept of virtual voices. One of the problems that often comes up early is the bleed that you can get from one sound area to another where you don't want it across walls for example so we'll look at a simple solution to what's called audio occlusion. One of the principles of sound designed for games is to be able to generate lots of variation in your sounds without necessarily requiring thousands of different sound files a so called procedural approach to sound design. We do this by breaking sound down into their components and then rebuilding the sounds with variation at runtime changing the sequence of the sounds using randomisation, layering sounds in different combinations and modulating sounds. We'll cover all this by developing some more advanced systems within sound cues. Sound cues are really useful and they're great at producing quick one off audio system solutions but once you get some basic concepts down you'll want to start experimenting with blueprints. With these you can begin building reusable systems of your own that have a little more complexity but will save you a lot of time in the long run. We'll show you how you can go from building a simple game trigger that just plays a sound when the player walks through it to a reusable ambient sound system that can spawn sounds randomly around the player's position. Once you've built this you've got a really adaptable sound blueprint that you can use in any game. By the end of the course you should have a really good understanding of interactive audio principles and you'll have the tools and knowledge to implement effective ambient sound into your games. You'll also hopefully have some inspiration about the kind of creative systems that you could go on to build in blueprints for your next project.
