Having sounds behave authentically within a game environment is just as important to giving a player a sense of presence as the graphics. We can start to support this sense of space by using the reverb and ambient zone settings of the audio volume actor to implement reverbs and occlusions that match the visual environment. The audio volumes are really built for rooms or fixed spaces. We've looked at how we can dynamically update them should the nature of these spaces change, but we've also looked at the attenuation occlusion system. Using raycasts this system is much more sophisticated and dynamic, working on an object level rather than a room level. You can include or exclude specific objects from using occlusion and since it's dynamic it will even work if these objects are moving. You've learnt how to choose different attenuation functions and how to make custom curves so that the attenuation of the sound over distance can be bespoke for the needs of each sound. Just dropping the volume over distance doesn't really do enough to replicate how sound operates in the real world and so we've looked at the air absorption options that allow us to filter the sound over distance as well. We've looked at spatialisation, how to use stereo sources in the game and when we might actually want to turn off spatialisation for large sound sources. In games the mix is always a challenge. If we try and play everything we end up with a muddy mix but deciding what's the most important sounds to the player at any one time can be difficult. The listener focus settings can be used to really subtly clarify the mix by attenuating sound sources that aren't the focus of the player's visual attention or it can be used for more dramatic effect to deliberately draw the player's attention to the sound sources that are of the greatest importance to them. If you haven't done it before you'll be amazed at how your soundscape can be transformed by introducing some movements of sounds through space. We've looked at a few techniques for this and you can copy the spline and vector-based blueprints to use in your own games. Finally we've looked at the camera and the listener, a crucial relationship which actually raises lots of questions and challenges. We don't know what will work best for your particular game but now you should have the knowledge to try out a few different options.
