Developing systems to communicate to your end users where they should be focusing to understand updated information is a crucial addition to a mixed reality experience. In this module, we will prepare our project in Unreal Engine to be ready for a specialized audio. We're going to look inside of the map that we will use for this module and see how it has been prepared. Then we will enable the spatialization plugin that is required by the HoloLens 2. Because we will be testing this from the Unreal Engine Editor, we will also need to make sure our workstation is prepared to play the specialized sounds. Let's get started inside of the sounds map located in world builder maps by course sound. This map has had a few instances of a blueprint placed throughout the scene which we will use to play some pre-recorded audio. I would recommend replacing the audio with your own as I'm sure it is more interesting than what we will demo here. For example, let's simulate the scene. Currently all the speakers are playing a sound trying to get you to look over at them, but one isn't saying, hey over here, it's trying to get your attention as there may be something to be concerned about. The audio is simply playing out of the speakers as any of your other sounds on your computer will play. Using only our ears, we will never know which of the speakers are playing the distracting over here sound and which one actually needs our attention. Even if I fly up close to one to try to hear if this is the one I need to pay attention to, it doesn't get any louder, nor does it get any quieter as I fly far away. What we need to do is prepare our sounds to respect our position and rotation in our world space and specialize the audio to react to those changes. We will further discuss this topic in the next module, so for now, let's get our project ready. Open the plugin settings and search for Microsoft Spatial Sound and enable it. This will require a restart and we have no other setting to change, so go ahead and do so. We now have all that we need, but must tell our HoloLens to use these new tools. Open project settings, scroll down and select HoloLens. At the bottom under audio, set the spatialization plugin to Microsoft Spatial Sound. As mentioned, we will be demoing all of this on Windows, so we also need to enable it there. Click on the volume icon in your taskbar. Select Spatial Sound and set it to Windows Sonic for headphones. This is only needed if you do intend to test it from your computer. With everything ready, we can move on to the next module where we will demonstrate the differences between Spatial and Traditional stereo audio and when to use each.
