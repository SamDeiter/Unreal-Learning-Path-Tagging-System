As we modify an Unreal Engine project to perform more optimally, we need to keep an eye on a few different metrics. In this module, we'll identify the basic hardware limiting factors that we must consider and how they can bottleneck performance when left unoptimized. At the start of a project, you should identify what hardware the end user will be using when they experience your Unreal Engine application. This will help you decide what your target frame rate will be. If you can't or shouldn't use any particular effects, or if there are any engine features that you simply won't need. Generally, mobile applications have more limitations for what features can be used because of the lower overhead for mobile hardware. For VR applications, your target frame rate should always be 90 frames per second. But for console and PC experiences, 60 frames per second is a common target frame rate. Here we are in an Unreal Engine project where we have a motorcycle with complex materials, lighting, and post-process effects. The motorcycle has been imported as a series of merged 3D objects that are, for the most part, unoptimized. To view the frame rate, type Tilda, stat FPS. In the viewport, we can see that our current frame rate is far below our target frame rate of 60 frames per second. We'll need to identify what is keeping our frame rate below our target of 60 frames per second, then find a way around that barrier. At any point, we may be experiencing bottleneck with our CPU, GPU, or memory usage. If you're developing on an expensive Mac or PC, you may want to keep in mind that your end user might not have access to the same type of hardware, so your bottleneck may differ from what theirs will be.
