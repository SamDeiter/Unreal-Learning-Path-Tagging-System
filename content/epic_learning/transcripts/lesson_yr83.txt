Adding reflections to your real-time scene is another integral piece of the photorealistic puzzle. The first way is to use baked reflections, which are generated using reflection capture actors that we can manually add to the level. The core benefit to reflection actors is that they have very little performance impact, but they do take up space and memory. So if you're working with limited memory, you'll need to be careful with how detailed or how large the textures generated by the reflection capture actors are. The other drawback is that these reflections aren't 100% accurate, depending on how you place them and from where they are viewed. Once all your reflection probes are in place, you can bake them by going to the build options and in choosing build reflection captures. We can also take advantage of screen space reflections or SSR. These are very accurate reflections and they're generated by the video cards GPU at runtime. They aren't baked so they don't take a mini texture memory, but they can have a huge impact on performance. Screen space reflections, unfortunately, only capture what's currently on the screen. Anything that is off screen will not be reflected in a reflective surface.
