Inherent in dynamic music is the potential for conflict between game events and musical structures. Most types of music rely upon a constant underlying pulse and metrical groupings of beats, and this alone creates patterns of expectations that can easily be interrupted by the unpredictability of gameplay timings. In this video we'll look at how you can make use of short, precinct or precynchronization sections of music to mitigate some of these issues. In games there are often climactic events which you want to feel rewarding to the player. Unlike in film, it's difficult to plan the music to hit these climatic moments since we don't know when they might happen. One solution is just to have the music change immediately using an unquantised transition. It is synchronised to the game event but can be musically clumsy unless the music is composed in such a way is to be ambiguous enough to tolerate such immediate transitions. Another more typical approach is to wait and transition the music at the next musical juncture, for example the next bar line. This is musically more satisfying but the music is not synchronised to the event itself. One way in which we can try to mitigate this problem is through the use of a precinct, a musical layer that reacts immediately to a game event and then leads us into the musical juncture. This allows us to acknowledge the event itself while not breaking the current musical pattern and gives us a forward trajectory towards a more musically satisfying transition point.
