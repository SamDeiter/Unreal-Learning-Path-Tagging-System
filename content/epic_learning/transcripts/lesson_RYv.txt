Hello everyone. Please open the section 1 contents project as we begin. While we load it up, let's talk about the generalities of what makes a blueprint a blueprint. The best way to think of a blueprint is as a container with certain structure. The container structure is described by the blueprint class. Within an actor class for example, we can store brushes, sounds, logic, and event calls so that when placed in our project they can be effectively autonomous. Even when explained in simple terms, their complexity can seem daunting but don't worry, soon it will be demystified. By now you should have the project loaded up so let's go ahead and get started with some simple practical examples. First, navigate to the folder section 1 underscore content, then to the null pointer folder. In here you will see a simple arrow asset and its two materials, direction null material and direction null material up. Now let's say we wanted our direction null to move forward while the one key is depressed on our keyboard. How exactly could we do this? Well first we will need a blueprint. Let's go ahead and make one. In the null pointer folder create yourself a new folder and name it BP for blueprint. Then open up that folder. Now by right clicking within this folder we can create a blueprint class. You can think of a class as your type of container like do you want a bucket, a box, a bottle. In this case we want to select an actor and name the created actor arrow. Congratulations you just made your first blueprint. Alright we now have an empty actor blueprint. Now let's go ahead and start making it do things. Once created we can now open the arrow blueprint we just created and start working on it. Let's do that now. You are now looking at the viewport tab in the blueprint. This is where you will be able to add assets and arrange them according to your wants. For now let's go ahead and drag in our direction null asset by simply clicking and dragging the asset into our blueprint. Once in we can now see that the arrow is there and that it is listed in the components pane. We could drag in another arrow if we wanted to. As you can see it's just like a miniature scene. We can also add other components to this by clicking on the add component drop down. Let's add a light. As you can see we can fill our actor container with all sorts of different things. Now let's go ahead and close our blueprint and drag in into our scene. Upon doing so we have now created an instance of our arrow blueprint. Let's go ahead and drag in another instance. Notice that we now have a pair of actors that share their appearance. They both have lights sticking out from up front and they both have two arrows each. If we go into the blueprint again and delete the extra arrow, both are instances update to this change. As you will see in future tutorials they can also share logic and even communicate with each other. But let's not get ahead of ourselves. What you should understand at this point is how to create an actor blueprint that they can contain things like models and lights and that they are instanceed when placed in the scene. Up next we will explore how blueprints can also share logic.
