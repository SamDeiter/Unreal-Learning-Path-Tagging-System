Time then for the final test of our course. Let's see if our material will actually switch as we interact with it. Coming to the top of the Unreal UI then, we can press the play level button. And, after walking through our still working automatic doors, we can make our way up the ramp towards the chair. Before we get too close though, let's come to a stop, aim the centre of our screen at the chair, and then click. The reason we want to stop here is because we know that our line trace distance has been set quite high. And so we just want to make sure that the material isn't able to change, unless the player has collided with the trigger box and set off the begin overlap event. Once we are happy that we can't actually change the material from here, we can move a little closer, and see if the text appears above the chair as we approach. We are doing this before clicking just to make sure that each part of the blueprint is working correctly. If we click as we approach for instance, we have no way of knowing whether or not the text queue is working. To make sure then, go slow when testing at first, and only perform one action at a time, as it will definitely make debugging easier. What we can do afterward of course is have some fun and try breaking the blueprint by clicking and jumping and running in and out of the collision box, all in rapid succession. Once we are happy that the text is working, we can click to change the applied material. Now it should be noted that we could make this blueprint more efficient by using child materials, commonly referred to as material instances, as they would be quicker to change at runtime than a standard unreal material. If we are going to have lots of objects in the level that can change material then, do be sure to use those. Keeping our beginner course simple however, still lets us see that swapping out one material for another works quite well. Let's make sure that all of this stops working when it should, by walking backwards while still clicking the mouse. And hopefully what we see will be the fact that once we are outside the trigger box, the material on the chair no longer changes. We could also walk in and out again, just to make sure that the text turns itself off even when we aren't clicking. And there we have it, yet another blueprint that can be used to enhance our real-time visualisations, should we want to add a little bit more interactivity to them.
