The Magic Leap 1, or simply Magic Leap, as it's often called, is a head-mounted display device which superimposes real-time 3D data over the real world. A virtual reality headset, such as the Oculus Rift and HTC Vive, block out the real world in favor of an entirely digital environment, which allows for a certain level of control over the player's experience. With an augmented reality experience, such as one for Magic Leap, you either build for a site-specific real-world environment, or you generalize the experience enough to be functional in a wide variety of real-world circumstances. Additionally, while many people have had exposure to augmented reality thanks to apps that utilize the camera on their mobile device, the Magic Leap 1, similarly to a VR headset, allows for 3D data to sustain an illusion of depth perception and presence by providing different views to your left and right eyes. So when might Magic Leap be a good choice for a project? If you'd like to see your 3D content in a spatial relationship with real-world context, Magic Leap may be a good choice. If you would like to manipulate and interact with that content using a full 6-degree of freedom controller or even your hands, Magic Leap may be a good choice. If you're creating an experience ideal for evenly lit indoor environments, and you'd like the freedom to move throughout those environments without being tethered to a large computer, Magic Leap may be a good choice. When might Magic Leap not be a good choice? Because Magic Leap is portable, the processing power of its attached computer, called the Light Pack, is limited compared to a device tethered to a desktop computer. The field of view of Magic Leap is also smaller than most VR headsets, so viewing objects on the device feels more natural than full environments. On projects where you need a robust level of processing power and immersion in a created 3D environment, a VR headset tethered to a high-end computer may be the best choice. Note that these are not hard and fast rules, and I recommend reviewing a series of guides Magic Leap has prepared on their website before designing any new experience.
