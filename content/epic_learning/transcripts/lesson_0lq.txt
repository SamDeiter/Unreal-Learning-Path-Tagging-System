Welcome to this class on packaging content with UE4. To begin, let's discuss what packaging is exactly. When you build your project in UE4, you utilize the editor to organize, assimilate, and control various types of media. You feed your project textures, material definitions, blueprints, C++ code, and a variety of other assets. This data proliferates throughout your project until you have a vast and complex culmination of code, data, levels, and assets. This is fantastic for working on your project, but what if you wanted to distribute your project to play in iOS, Android, Linux, or even another PC without the editor? This is where packaging comes in. When you package a project, you effectively make a playable version of your project that does not require the editor. In doing so, UE4 will take all the different elements of the game and reformat them for efficient playback on a specified system. The process is generally painless and unless you have major errors in your project, it should work fairly flawlessly. Once complete, your project executable will be substantially lighter in UE, will have dramatically fewer files, and will be much easier to transport and distribute than the project, editor, and all of the accompanying files. For virtually every project, a packaged executable is the intended final output of your project, and is the expected method for making your project distributable.
