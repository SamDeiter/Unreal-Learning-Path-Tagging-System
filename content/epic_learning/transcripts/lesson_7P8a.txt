With our importance volume in place from the previous exercise then, time to take a look at light maps next and consider how they can affect the quality of lighting in our scene when it comes time to bake out our indirect illumination, a topic that will be covered in the light mobility section of this course. At this point, what we really need to know is just what light maps are. Well, in short, they are an unreal way of storing or adding indirect lighting to each static mesh in a scene, remembering of course that Unreal currently has no ability to produce a real-time dynamic GI effect. To add good looking GI to a scene then, Unreal bakes all of the lighting information down to bitmaps that for clarity's sake are called light maps. Because these bitmaps are applied to scene geometry, they will naturally need to have a set of UV coordinates made available so that they know how to map themselves to objects, coordinates that we would typically want to keep separate from any texturing UVs that may be applied. Some best practice methods for getting good resolution out of light maps in a scene would include not having overlapping UVs in the light map set. We would also want to ensure that each UV island has sufficient padding, the lower the resolution of the light map, the more padding required. And we would also want to decide which mesh objects in the scene can get by using just low resolution maps and which would need high resolution ones. To bake the lighting information that we have into these light maps, we can come to the top of our 3D view, click on the build button and then click to select the build lighting on the option. Focusing in on the headboard area of the bed where we can see that the lamps are casting a shadow and just keep an eye on things as I make a change to the headboard's light map resolution. Coming to the details tab then, we want to expand the lighting rollout if it isn't already and then put a check in the overridden light map res checkbox. Let's set the headboard to a resolution of 512 in this instance as all resolutions need to be a power of 2 and then re-bake the lighting. And as you can see, making that resolution change has definitely improved the quality of our baked lighting solution. Although an even better example of this can be shown by taking a look at the bed sheet here. Go ahead and select that, setting its light map resolution override to a value of 1024 and then re-bake the lighting again. With the improvement that we see now, hopefully speaking for itself. Now typically this selective improvement would be applied to all objects in a scene in order to optimise our light maps as much as possible. As artists and our developers then we may need to determine which objects will be seen clearly in a level and at what kinds of distance they will be viewed so that we can alter light map resolutions accordingly. Of course, light map resolution isn't the BR and end all of lighting quality in a scene and so be sure to hold off ramping the overrides up too high for now as other factors may be contributing to any lack of lighting quality that we are currently seeing, factors that we will be taking a look at a little later on. That having been said though, light map resolution can very obviously have a big impact on the quality of final lighting in our visualisation pieces. And so when producing just a single set environment such as we have here, we may want to try pushing the settings a little higher than we perhaps would if we were creating something like a game.
