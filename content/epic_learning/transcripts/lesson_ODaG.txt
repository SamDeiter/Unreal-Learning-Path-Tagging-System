Welcome back to the R of Code series and in this module we'll learn about the modeling mode that's built into Unreal Engine. The modeling mode is a suite of tools that Unreal provides so that you don't need to leave the engine to make even highly complex custom models. It should be noted however that this feature is still in beta and is best suited for gearboxing and blocking our geometry or creating simple environment assets. Where you'll find the most amount of value in using the modeling mode isn't quickly prototyping ideas and blocking out levels. Basic but complete world building before you flesh out the details in a dedicated 3D modeling software. If you had an idea that you wanted to first test out with your character and see if the level design is sound and consistent with your vision for the game, the modeling mode you'll find is a powerful tool to have under your belt. So with that said, let's create a mini 3D platformer. An obstacle course that our little character here has to make his way through. If you're coming here from the Blueprint module, you'll see that I've made a few changes from what the level used to look like. It's nothing crazy. I just wanted to fill the void up a little and everything you see here are assets that you can find within the project. So I encourage you to go into folders such as learning it games and learning it robots to find assets to hash out your level. We have quite a few assets at our disposal but today we're going to look at what your options are if you need an asset that is unique to your liking and is so custom that you need to design it yourself. We'll be designing two obstacles. The first is a revolving set of rocks that players must jump through and the second is a series of rotating propellers or fans where players will need to jump from blade to blade. These should be simple enough to implement while still introducing you to the core tools and techniques of the modeling mode. Without further ado, let's jump straight into it.
