To help us get up and running with Blueprints, it would probably be good for us to spend a little time on the basics of just what Blueprints are inside Unreal. Blueprints are on reals way of making scripting our coding as easy as possible for beginners to use, in that it is a set of visual nodes that can be placed inside a Blueprint graph and then connected together in such a way as to create gameplay or interactivity. And though we ourselves may not be a coder or scripter of any sort. Interactivity can be as simple as creating a working like switch, but can easily go on to more complex effects, such as giving users the ability to move furniture around in a building or even swap out static meshes in a scene, essentially changing the objects that inhabit the visualization level. Besides being powerful, Blueprints are also extremely flexible, which is perfect for an environment in which project requirements can be for ever changing. In a nutshell then, this is a visual scripting system that allows people who don't want to learn how to code, per se, to still have access to tools and features that are usually only available to coders. Although just a simple introduction to what Blueprints are, even from this simple description, I'm sure that you, as a visual designer, can see the potential of making Blueprints a part of your visualization skill set.
