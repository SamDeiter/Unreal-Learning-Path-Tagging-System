We'll talk about terminology and environment art within game production and Unreal. We'll talk about folder structure and file naming, importing meshes, textures and assets into Unreal properly, using the grid and planning with modular workflows, texture settings, density and usage, block out meshes and understanding mesh footprints, material creation, collision setup, optimization and a little bit of lighting overview and about light maps. This class is intended for entry level and junior environment artists that are beginning to get into development of environments using the Unreal Engine. We assume we know the basics of UI and layout of Unreal with where the primary buttons are located and controls are, but we'll be discussing and going over many of the features, discussed in detail, and we'll have some recap on some fundamental practices and controls in Engine. We've divided this course into three main sections. Section one will be our first steps into our course curriculum and this will focus mostly on pre-productions and ways to prepare your workflow and mindset for working as an environment artist. Section two will be focused on some of the more important tools you'll be using as an environment artist in the Unreal Engine. And section three will be focused mostly on materials and lighting and how those features in Unreal play an important part to the development and production process. Now at the end of this course, you should have a good understanding of the tools and processes used and required for production as an environment artist working in the Unreal Engine.
