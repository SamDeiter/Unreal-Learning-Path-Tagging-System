Welcome to AnimationHub. In this tutorial, we're going to take a look at vectors for stylized comic materials. Let's get to it. In the content browser, right click and create a material. Double click the material to open the material editor. Set the metallic setting to one. Set the specular setting to zero and set the roughness setting to one as well. Click in the graph area and press the tab key and type sky atmosphere light direction and hit the enter key to generate the node. Click the graph area and press tab key and type pixel normal world space and hit the enter key to generate that node. Drag from the outpin of the sky atmosphere light direction node and type do to generate the dot operator node. Connect the outpin of the pixel normal world space to the B pin of the product node. Now dragging from the outpin of the dot operator node, type power and hit the enter key to generate that power node. Drag from the outpin of the power node and type multiply and hit the enter key to generate the multiply node. Drag from the multiply node and type subtract and hit the enter key to add the subtract node. For the power multiply and subtract nodes, rightclick and promote to parameter for each of the open settings. Connect the out pin of the subtract node to your base color and emissive settings on the material. To see the results, save the material and close the graph. In the content browser, rightclick the material and select create material instance. Drag the material instance onto the mesh in the level. Double click the material instance to adjust the parameters of the material to your liking. Now the material can be extended to have the best of both vectors. In the graph area, press the tab key and generate a camera vector whirlpace node. Drag off of the sky atmosphere light vector outpurp and hit the enter key to generate a linear interpolation node. Connect the camera vector world space to the B slot of the linear interpolation node. Right click on the alpha pin and promote to parameter. Name this vector blend. And this will allow for blending to and from each of the different vectors. Either the vector coming from the light or the vector coming from the camera. That's it. See you next time.
