Levels of detail or LODs refer to the geometric complexity of a 3D model. As the camera in Unreal Engine moves away from a 3D object, we can render less complex versions of 3D geometry to improve optimization. When the camera in Unreal Engine is close to a 3D object, you typically want that geometry to have more polygons. When the camera is farther away, you typically don't need as many polygons to render that object. LODs are different versions of the same 3D model that have varying amounts of detail that can be used depending on how far away the viewer is from the object. In the viewport, switch to the quad-overdraw optimization view mode that we previously discussed in this course. We learned that areas of high polygon density are more expensive to render because pixels where multiple polygons overlap take much longer to render. As we move the camera out, we can see that while our geometry isn't changing, it's becoming more dense and therefore less efficient to render. With levels of detail, we can make that geometry less dense at a distance without sacrificing visual equality.
