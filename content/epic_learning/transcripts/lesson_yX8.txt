Before we finish this module, I wanted to talk about lighting motivations and how the different light actors we've just talked about can kind of fall into these categories. Those four categories, by the way, are environmental, emulated, practicals, and cheated light. Environmental light, as the name suggests, is natural light that is meant to come from the world outside. So this is primarily sunlight and sky ambience, but it can be a few other things like moon light. Emulated light is kind of a live action crossover light theory here. What I mean by that is that it's when we place different light actors to accentuate and emulate both the environmental and practical light sources. So we won't always be in a position where we are having a light genuinely be sunlight, we may want it to pretend to be sunlight just for a specific shot. And so it's good practice to kind of keep those two mentally separated. Practicals in this context are real world light sources that generate light and can be seen in the frame. So that's going to be lamps, light bulbs, things in that things of that nature. The last category, cheated light here, is really just all the other light sources that don't fall into the above three categories. The reason I'm calling it cheated light is because if it doesn't fall into those three top categories, it is kind of something that probably isn't physically possible or physically accurate, but may still do something cinematic for your scene.
