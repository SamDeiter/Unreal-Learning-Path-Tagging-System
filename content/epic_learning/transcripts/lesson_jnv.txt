Hello and welcome to the Mixing In Effects course. I'm Richard and I'll be doing the talking in these videos but the course has been put together by myself and my colleague Dave Raybold. You can follow Dave on Twitter at the new Tide. We've been working together a long time and have written two books on Unreal Engine Audio, the fabled Game Audio Tutorial and the more recent Game Audio implementation. We also write papers and book chapters on Game Audio and teach the sound and music for interactive games, Master's Course, at Leeds Beckett University. We keep an up to date list of useful books and reading around the subject matter at our website, GameAudio Implementation.com. This course is all about mixing and effects, so you'll need to be familiar with an audio editor in order to produce sound or music assets to bring into a game. We're also going to assume that you have some familiarity with the engine and so would recommend these courses. And we'll assume that you've done the basic introductory courses on audio in Unreal Engine. First the course on ambient and procedural sound design and secondly the course on dynamic audio. We'll be relying upon several concepts covered already in these courses, so if you haven't already completed them then we'd recommend you do so before proceeding. Throughout the course we'll be referring to systems that we've built in a demonstration game project. You can download this project to take a look at things in more detail and to see the systems in action. In this course you'll be learning how to set up sound class hierarchies for controlling the audio mix between different groups of sounds by either triggering sound class mixes, automating mixes, or using modifiers or overrides. We'll then look at how to apply effects to sounds in response to game events, using source effects, dynamic bus sends, sub mix effects, and sub mix sends. And then we'll be looking at how to alter these effects dynamically. After looking at how to implement specialist effects like convolution reverb and sidechain compression, we'll finish by looking at how the mix of a game is dependent upon more than just the volume of sounds. And we'll consider issues like prioritisation and concurrency. By the end of the course you'll have a good understanding of how to implement dynamic mixes and effects within your game.
