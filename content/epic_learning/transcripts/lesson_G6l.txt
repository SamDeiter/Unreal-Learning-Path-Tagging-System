Welcome to the stylized rendering course. My name is Chris Kaufman and I've been working as a creative professional in games, film, education, and television since 2002. Over the duration of this course, we'll examine a number of ways to customize our level's render to generate non-photoreal stylized results. We'll examine the theory behind what makes stylized aesthetics work, from project preparation to the final render settings. We'll take a close look at how to implement flat materials for a graphic cartoon look, outlines on all our levels actors and on select objects, high contrast lighting that will make the default lights appear much more graphic, and custom color bands that will further stylize the lights transition from light to dark. We'll also discover how to implement a painterly effect on the environment and the characters. By the end of this course, we'll have identified a number of techniques to aid us in the creation of our own stylized levels.
