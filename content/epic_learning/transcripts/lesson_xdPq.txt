Baking light maps can take a great deal of time. Obviously, the larger the scene, the higher the quality settings, and depending upon your light mass settings, the more the time grows. Light map begs can often take entire days to bake for really large scenes. This is where having a render farm at your disposal helps, but a render farm doesn't have to be an actual render farm. It could be as simple as having a few more machines at home or on your work network. It goes without saying that each PC should be of fairly moderate to high-end capabilities and they should pretty much match in capabilities. You don't want to have a network of machines where several of the machines are much lower quality. As these lower quality machines can actually slow down your build times. To bake your light maps on a distributed network like this, you'll need to set up and use Unreal's swarm. You'll find the swarm application in your Unreal Engine installation. To go on Windows under C and then program files, you'll find the Epic Games folder. In here, you'll have all of your different versions of the Unreal Engine. I'm using version 4.19. From here, we'll go into the Engine directory, then into binaries, and finally into .NET. The two applications in here that we'll be using are swarm agent.exe and swarm coordinator.exe. Swarm coordinator only needs to be run on the main computer that will be coordinating the light map build jobs. In this case, this is my office computer and it will be the swarm coordinator. You'll need to run swarm agents on all other machines, including the coordinator machine if you want to use it as a build machine as well. After swarm coordinator has started up, it will begin to list the machines on the network that have swarm agent running.
