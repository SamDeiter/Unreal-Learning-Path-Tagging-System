We will also go over some of the limitations we might encounter at this time. First are requirements. Nanite requires a Nvidia Maxwell or newer GPU or an AMD GCN or newer GPU, as well as DirectX 12. Well, let's get started. How do we enable Nanite? We're going to first import a mesh and go from there. I'm importing a normal mesh using our normal methods, and we have a build Nanite checkbox at the top. When we enable this in import, it's going to go to import that stack mesh and convert it over to a Nanite mesh. Here we can see our mesh has been imported, and if we look at the details, we notice it says Nanite enabled true. And if we open this up, we can find that we have enabled Nanite support checked. This is the other method if you already have meshes in your scene and you wish to switch them over to Nanite, you can open them up in the stack mesh editor and enable Nanite support. Alternately, you can right click on any stack mesh in your browser, go to the top and enable or disable Nanite. Now what should we be using Nanite for? Nanite is great for stack meshes and geometry collections. Nanite supports dynamic translation, rotation, and non-uniform scaling of rigid meshes, but it does not support general mesh deformation, whether it's dynamic or static. We should be trying to use on stack meshes that contain many triangles or have triangles that will be very small on screen, something we have many instances of in our scene, or if it acts as a major occluder of other Nanite geometry. What should Nanite not be used with? Nanite currently does not work with skeletal meshes, you cannot use custom death or stencil buffers. Vertex painting on instances of Nanite meshes will not work, however vertex colors can be imported from the original mesh and are supported. Now what about our limitations for both materials used for rendering and rendering in general? So when using materials any blend mode besides opaque will not work. You're going to need to use opaque for your blend mode, defer decals or not going to work, and wireframe mode. For material outputs, the world position offset and the pixel depth offset are not supported and you cannot use two-sided rendering. In terms of rendering, the forward shading renderer is not supported, stereo rendering, which is primarily used for VR, as well as split screen are both not supported with Nanite meshes. MSAA is not supported as well as it requires forward shading and forward shading does not work. Lighting channels inside of your meshes will not work, by default is going to use channel zero, the others will not be supported. And lastly ray tracing does not work against the fully detailed Nanite mesh, but it does trace against the proxy mesh. And you might be asking, well what is a proxy mesh? Many parts of Unreal Engine need access to the traditional vertex buffer provided by traditionally rendered meshes. When Nanite is enabled for stack mesh, it generates a coarse representation that is accessible and used where the highly detailed Nanite data cannot be. In those cases, the proxy mesh is the generated representation used, like when complex collisions needed or when a platform doesn't support Nanite rendering. Let's go ahead and look at the proxy mesh information. Here we can see our Nanite mesh and on the top left we can see we have around 180,000 Nanite triangles, but under this we have fallback triangles, it's about 1800. We can visualize our proxy mesh by going to show Nanite fallback and then it's going to switch over to our proxy mesh. And you can see we have a much rougher representation of our item since we have significantly less triangles. We can also view this in wireframe mode and we can see our fallback proxy mesh and its amount of details. Switch back to unchecking Nanite fallback to see the Nanite version and you can see we have our Nanite which has many more triangles and a much smoother representation. The proxy mesh is very important because like I mentioned before, anything that doesn't support Nanite, for example mobile or certain console platforms, will use that proxy mesh only, it will not use the Nanite mesh. If you find your proxy mesh is not meeting your needs, it's too rough, you have options inside of here such as the keep triangle percent, how much of the original triangle we keep for the Nanite mesh and the fallback triangle percent, which is how much our proxy mesh will use of the original mesh. In addition, one last thing inside of here, let's say you're using a Nanite mesh and you don't know where you want to use it or you want to use it for a different usage, you can uncheck the enable Nanite support and it will do its best to convert it back over to a normal stack mesh without Nanite support.
