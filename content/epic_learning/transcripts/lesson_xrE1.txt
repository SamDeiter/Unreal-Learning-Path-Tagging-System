We've taken a look at how we can use the PCG framework system to define assets and rules and create worlds for us based on those rules. We also took some of the prebuilt content that came with the project and made around starting point for our own environment. There are quite a few other interesting items inside this project that you might want to take a look at as well. Inside of a project, in addition to our environment maps, we have a few of the breakdown maps. For example, inside of our large assembly breakdown map, we can see our large assembly created and the graph used as well as the different pieces that were created in order to make our large assembly. By adjusting those different pieces, we could actually create our own large assembly and this breakdown map shows us how. In one of our fly-throughs, you may have noticed something standing out of the ordinary. Inside of our electric dreams, underscore environment map, off to the side, we have a funny little mesh all by itself. This mesh is a sample of using the substrate material system in order to layer different materials together to give a nice unique look. You can open up that material and take a look at how it was created. Taking this apart would be a great way to learn how you can use substrate in future materials. In addition, if you were listening to the sample environment when you were play testing, you might have noticed we have some audio also set up. Here's a complete MetaSoundsSoundscape system which you can open up and explore to see how MetaSounds is used to bring this environment to life. Procedural content generation is a set of tools that enable us to define the rules and parameters we want to use to populate large scenes using the assets of our choice. While these tools are currently in experimental, you can see just how powerful they are to help you speed up your workflow and let us get our projects created more efficiently and effectively.
