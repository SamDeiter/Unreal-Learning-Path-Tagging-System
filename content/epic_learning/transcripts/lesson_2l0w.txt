We've gone through the basics of the material system inside of Unreal Engine. We looked at the editor, created a few different types of materials, and explored the different options available to us when setting up our materials. So what do we do next? I would continue doing what we have been doing by playing with the project you might have opened now and exploring. Change the textures that we used. Adjust the tiling. Try and make a basic scene. Change the material that lets you not only adjust the tiling of your texture, but maybe also the color and roughness of any of your instances. Next I would go over the different samples available to you on the launcher. There are some pretty good ones in here, including what I've mentioned before, the content examples maps. We have multiple maps available to you inside of the content examples, going over subjects such as more advanced topics, instances, the different nodes in more detail, as well as advanced properties. I go to the Epic Developer Community site. There's more material content there from both the developer community as well as Epic games. The docs pages are full of information and more technical data related to the different uses of materials and the different properties available to you, such as the different shading models we didn't cover in depth. Materials are a large part of Unreal Engine. They can play a major role in providing opportunities for adding that extra polish and life to your creations. When you're ready to learn more, there's plenty of learning resources available to you to help you bring your creativity to life.
