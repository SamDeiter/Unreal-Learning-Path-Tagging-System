Ever since we added the post process volume to our scene, we have in fact been viewing a small amount of bloom in the view as we have been working through this core. And as with exposure, bloom can be used to change how we perceive the lighting that we are looking at, meaning we can easily use this to our advantage. Searching far and then selecting the post process volume in the world outline of then, let's come to the lens section and expand the bloom rollout. Here, we can put a check in the Intensity and the Threshold values. And as I'm sure that you don't need me to tell you what the intensity setting does, let's crank this up to a value of 105. A little on the extreme side, I will admit, but it really lets us see how we can limit the bloom's effect to certain areas of our visualization, or more accurately, to just the brightest areas if we want. So notice what happens as we adjust the threshold setting closer to a positive 1 value. The bloom effect is now severely limited as to where it shows up. In fact, we can produce an almost dreamlike look to our visualization by whiting out the windows whilst not overpowering the fog on the interior of our room, by using the threshold value of around about 0.7. Notice how the windows and directs on areas of the scene are blown out with bloom whilst the rest of the room is still very visible. Just another tool then that we can use to craft the exact lighting experience that we want. And remember, we can go from the extreme as we have here to the subtle in that we just add a nice soft quality to our lighting.
