First up, we'll briefly talk about what it means to be a gameplay designer. What is expected of you in the team, but more importantly to this course, what is expected of you when working inside UE4? When we look at different job applications for the role of a gameplay designer, Lucky for us, a lot of the same points come up. You should be able to produce concepts for new gameplay items and systems, look for areas of improvements with the current design. You should be able to author and maintain design documents, be able to work collaboratively in the team, and be able to make quick iterations. Blueprints are the perfect tool to help you produce new prototypes for gameplay or to iterate on something that a programmer has already made. The uniform and consistent look of Unreal Engine also make it easier to step outside your area of expertise and have a look at other stepwork, be able to understand what an artist is doing. As a gameplay designer, you should have basic knowledge on most of the different features of Unreal. You should be comfortable with the mesh viewer, materials, sounds, and other features the Engine has to offer. Knowing a bit about these other features will make it a lot easier to work together with other departments. So in summary, your responsibilities inside the Engine are having a basic understanding of most features of the Engine, knowing how to quickly iterate on gameplay elements, and being able to create new variations or completely new prototype actors for your project.
