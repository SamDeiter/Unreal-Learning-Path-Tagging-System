Having already added lights to our scene, we have made a good start at creating the lighting effect that we are aiming for. As on real is a real time engine however, there are some extra steps that we will need to take if we are going to get the most out of our lighting setup, especially so when it comes to making use of indirect lighting effects such as global illumination. The first tool that we will look at in order to help take our lighting further at this stage then is the light mass importance value. What is that? And why do we need one you may ask? Well from inside the modes tab of the UI we can either search for the word volume in the search field or we can come to the volume section and then drag an importance volume into the scene from there. Where will we want to place this? Essentially, anywhere in the scene that we will need more than just direct lighting, which typically will be wherever the player or client or user will spend time looking around. Now whilst multiple importance volumes can be placed in a scene, Unreal will in the end encompass all of them inside an invisible bounding box that will calculate bounce light for all areas of the scene within its boundaries. And because of this we will generally want to avoid having a number of volumes spread out across a level with large gaps in between them and given that in the end we won't be saving on calculation times in any way at all. What we do want to do here then is make sure that our volume is encompassing all of the scene's geometry. To do that let's set the volume scale values to 7, 7 and 4 respectively, after which we can tweak its position in the scene if needed. A question that you will probably want answering at this point of the proceedings is, what exactly does a light mass importance volume do? Simply put, it helps focus light calculations in Unreal so that we can create multiple bounces of light in a scene without unnecessarily impacting the engine's calculation times. Without this volume we would only get one bounce of indirect light, which is the light mass default for non-importance volume areas in a level. Volume also gives us the ability to add volume lighting samples to specific areas of a level that can open up possibilities of creating certain lighting effects, such as giving the impression that indirect illumination in the scene is lighting up any animated objects or characters that we have. To see these samples in view we can come to the 3D view, click on the show button and then come to the visualized fly out and click to enable the volume lighting samples option. As noted then, having this importance volume in place can both help us create multiple bounces of light in an environment as well as focus our lighting calculations so that when it comes time to bake our solution down to light maps, which we will look at in a few videos time, we can create the lighting build in as short a time as possible. This however is only one part of the puzzle in terms of creating good quality lighting in a real, as there are in fact two other factors that can greatly change how effective this lighting importance volume can be, one of which we will look out in the next video.
