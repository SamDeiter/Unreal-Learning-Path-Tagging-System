Another way in which we can control how a client sees lighting inside an unreal visualization piece would be by making use of a lookup table. These can be thought of as a post-processing style guide that tells a real what kind of picture adjustments be they levels, curves, exposure or tinting to apply to a camera within a post-processing volume that has a lot applied to it. Lookup tables can be purchased are even sometimes given away as free downloads that can be found with an internet search for the word lot. What we're going to do here however is take a look at how we can create our own using the Photoshop application. Let's imagine that our client wants a very specific look for their project. Inside Photoshop we have grabbed and added a screenshot of our unreal scene via the print screen command. Meaning we could now for instance apply an existing color lookup that comes with Photoshop. And so let's select the crisp winter preset found in the 3D look file drop down. We do need to bear in mind here that we could just as easily add curves, levels and tint of our own and the look creation process that we will show now would work just the same. What we need to do next is open up the natural color lot that can be grabbed from the unreal documentation page. And then drag and drop the adjustment layers that we have created onto it. After which we can save this out as a PNG file, calling it winter underscore LUT. Or whatever file name we like. Inside Unreal we can then click import, locate the looked file and bring it in. After double clicking the image we will want to make sure that in the level of detail tab we are not generating mip maps and that the texture group is set to color lookup table. After which we can click save. Let's then search for our post process volume inside the world outliner, come to the color grading rollout and in here expand the miscellaneous rollout, putting a check in the color grading look check box. After expanding the drop down let's search for our winter underscore LUT file that we saved out of Photoshop and then click to add it. As we move around the scene now and turn the LUT on and off, we can clearly see how we are able to completely change the look and feel of our lighting using just a simple .png look file. Which of course opens up a huge array of possibilities when it comes to crafting the perfect lighting solution for our clients.
