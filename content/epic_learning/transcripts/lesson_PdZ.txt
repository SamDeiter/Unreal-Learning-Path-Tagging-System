Team sizes matter as they directly relate to how much work and what type of work you'll be doing day to day as an environment artist. Each studio will have a certain flow or process as to what tasks come to you and what your responsibilities will be and this can often be directly related to how big a team is and what resources are available internally for development of the project. For instance, a AAA larger studio would generally provide you with a specific task that are very confined to a skill set or aspect of the game. You might be the artist that works on all the trees or bushes or the artist that focus on building modular pieces or the artist that makes the tie-able texture sets. This is also the way you'll continue to learn and get better at that specific vein of skills and produce high-end results with extreme focus. A nice perk to AAA studios as well is that you have a lot of other teams and co-workers as resources to help you problem solve or figure out issues. A potential downside for some is that you won't be going out of your skill bubble much and trying a lot of newer things and you'll most likely be focused on those specific tasks that you mentioned. For a slightly smaller studio like an indie team or a AA developer, the team sizes will be reduced and their resources slightly more limited due to this as well. This kind of position will be more open to your tasking and you'll be wearing more hats throughout production and working on more aspects of the project because of this. Generally in this position as an environment artist, you'll be creating and working with most if not all aspects of environment production ranging from foliage, buildings, props, textures, materials, landscapes, you name it. This is a great fit for someone that likes the structure of a full company but the ability to try a lot of things and be more flexible with how they spend their day working as you'll be moving to multiple tasks throughout production. Lastly, a super small team like a startup or a simple work group will also be dramatically different than the other two options here. A team like this can be anywhere from one to 25 members big and your responsibilities will be very ranged as you'll be responsible for many if not all aspects of the environment art and potentially even other art departments.
