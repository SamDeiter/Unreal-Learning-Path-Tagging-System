Now that we have a good idea of what we will learn in this course and the learning path in general, let us get started by learning what MR and by extension XR is. Augmented reality, virtual reality, mixed reality, what is the difference? And how do we know which to use and when? One of the great things about this new avenue for developing projects that blend reality is that defining the three of these is getting more and more difficult. Saying that this is getting difficult is strange to describe as a great thing, but the more the boundaries between them blur, the more immersive and interactable our projects can become. We have all been excited to see the development of this technology increase in usage and popularity with many incredible use cases. Starting out, let us simply define these three to a very high level. Virtual reality is another reality. It is not shared with us and we need tools to not only interact with objects in this reality, but we also need to wear a headset to be placed inside. Projects that work in virtual reality are exciting because they allow us to experience possibilities not available to us in our own reality. We can extend the more traditional immersion from an experience played out on a flat monitor and instead virtually dive in to be placed alongside and take part in the action. Augmented reality is the alteration of our own reality. It allows us to place objects or other worlds into our own environments to learn from, interact with or just be. The potential for augmented reality uses is growing constantly from gaming and storytelling to instruction and exploration. The benefit to augmented reality is that it is a separate reality overlaid onto our own. The potential for this technology has only just begun to be realized and is growing rapidly. Windows mixed reality and similar XR systems, taking the best of both virtual and augmented reality. I feel that Microsoft stated it clearer than I can. Mixed reality is a blend of physical and digital worlds, unlocking the links between human, computer and environment interaction. XR, however, is not a technology itself. It is the umbrella term consisting of the various technologies we just discussed. As the lines between conventional reality and computer interaction blends, the further our immersion becomes. Our perception of what is real and in front of us is blurred, thus creating a mixed reality, bringing our projects and worlds and experiences out of the confines of the screen and letting them live out in our world. I highly recommend diving deeper into this space as it is very fascinating to see where we are headed.
