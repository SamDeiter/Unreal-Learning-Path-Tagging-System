Without this solution, we found that many of our shots were too heavy to work with, just due to the sheer size and number of the effect sequences. In addition to this sprite sheet, we stored information about how the image was cropped so that it could be accurately replicated in Unreal. Once it's imported into Unreal, we used our custom effects projection tool to align the effects with the camera. This tool converts the screen space coordinates from 2D sequence into 3D space, allowing for depth layering and seamless integration with the 3D environment. Doing this ensured a pixel accurate projection. Unlike traditional sprite cards, this approach gives us precise control over where the effects sit in the frame or in the screen space without distorting the image. Each layer of the effects can be positioned to the correct depth, maintaining the illusion of 2D animation while blending with the 3D world. By integrating 2D hand-drawn effects into Unreal with the projection system, we've maintained the traditional look while gaining the flexibility to iterate directly within the engine. This allows for faster views, consistency across shots, and precise compositing of 2D effects in a 3D environment. So where to next?
