Welcome to our Learning Through Examples class, where we will start to learn the use of Blueprints via four practical examples. Although we will first of all take some time out to learn a little bit about Blueprints themselves, so what they are and how we might use them before diving into the first of our examples, which will be the creation of an automatic sliding door, an example that will give us our first look at creating triggers, timelines and variables. Next up we will learn how to create a light switch with exposed parameters that will allow us to connect it to as many or as few static metalite actors as we please. This example also teaches us how to construct a line trace and fire off custom events. In our third example we will be learning about construction scripts, seeing how these can potentially save us huge amounts of time when building a level. In this particular instance we will see how we can grab an actor's current location in the scene and then use a line trace node. After which we will be able to snap it to a surface that sits above once it comes within a certain tolerance distance that we will also set. Finally, we will look at how we can give a user the ability to swap out an object's material whilst the level is running, taking the time also to make sure that this ability is limited to working only when the player is stuck close to and looking directly at the meshing question using visibility nodes, triggers, variables and line traces again. Let's get going.
