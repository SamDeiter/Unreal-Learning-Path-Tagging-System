We've covered a lot of techniques in this course, focusing on how you can make your audio dynamically respond to game states, conditions and variables. Understanding and using Boolean, integer and float variables will definitely feel like a big learning curve if you've not come across these kind of things before, but they hold the power to make your audio truly responsive to gameplay. Using timelines, using curves and mapping and manipulating numbers all take some work to get your head around, but with patience and methodical approach and plenty of cups of tea, you will get there. And once you do, you'll see the huge potential to implement your ideas. The great opportunity with Unreal Engine and with Blueprints is that after you've become familiar with it, you can pretty much get on and do things yourself. You don't have to be waiting for a programmer to help you out. The challenge with audio is that to do it well, you need to know how pretty much everything else in the game works. But this is also the brilliant thing about audio. You really understand the game you're working on and you could be part of the design process. With everything you've learnt in this course, you could probably make a game yourself. Why not have a go?
