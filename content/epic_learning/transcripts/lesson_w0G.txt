Whether it is software on your desktop, on a mobile device, a headset, or anything else, it needs to be tested. As the designers and developers of our projects, it becomes very easy to miss something that we didn't consider, didn't experience, or have become numb to. Therefore, testing by users outside of your team is crucial. In this module, we are going to be talking about best practices regarding one of the most common issues users have with reality altering experiences, motion sickness, and what we can do to help them avoid it. We are influencing what our end users will see and how they will interact with this new blended reality, and as such, we have a responsibility not to overwhelm their systems, their devices, and their own perception. We need to recognize that there will be users who are altogether uncomfortable using a device such as the HoloLens 2, as it makes them feel unwell. As we plan to build projects for HoloLens, keep in mind a few best practices to minimize the chance of causing motion sickness in your end users. Let's start with the primary means of interacting with HoloLens, our eyes. Of all the objects in the system, the pieces we need to take most care of is the user's eyes. When mistaken our development that forces strain on the eyes will be an uncomfortable rejection of the project or even the device itself. The HoloLens is prepared to render objects in our scenes correctly for each eye, but it is our job to make sure things are placed in the right spot for the most natural viewing.
