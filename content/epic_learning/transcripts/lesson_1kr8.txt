Hey there, my name is Kyle Chinden. Currently I'm a lead animator in the games industry, as well as an adjunct professor at the University of Texas at Austin, where I teach animation courses. For the past 10 years I've worked at both indie and AAA studios, developing titles for PC, consoles, VR and mobile, many of them utilizing Unreal Engine. The animation world is constantly changing. There are animators who focus solely on the craft itself, but there are also an increasing number of animators who are diving into the larger ecosystem of game development, thanks to the ease of use with engines such as UE5. Within this course, we'll touch on a broad range of topics, including animation aliases, retargeting, attaching a weapon to a character, using montages, notifies, blend spaces, creating a crouch and uncrouch state, and then ultimately wrapping it up by adjusting existing animations using control rig. For this course, some prior experience with Unreal Engine is recommended, such as being comfortable with general animation systems in the editor. The tools animators commonly utilized within Unreal Engine are pretty simple to start playing around with, and can result in some big differences on screen. We will start by looking at the more foundational animation tools within the engine, then take those tools and tweak them to create more complex content. By the end of this course, you should have a solid understanding of the most commonly used tools animators work with within the engine, and how they interact together. As a result, you'll end up with a playable character that can run around, crouch, and both holster and unholster a weapon. This concludes the introduction of this course.
