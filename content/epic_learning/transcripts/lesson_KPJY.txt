Understanding how the various parts of Unreal's content pipeline work is critical and ensuring that you and members of your team don't have issues when it comes to dealing with custom content. The first thing that I'd like to touch about in the content pipeline is the unit of measurement Unreal uses, but Real 5 uses Cinometers for its unit of measure. So one Unreal's unit is going to equal one Cinometer. The thing that you set your digital content creation system to Cinometers will ensure that you avoid any and all scaling issues that might incur on your project. While you can set your exporter to scale your model up or down, oftentimes people forget this, especially as projects come to an end. And this one simple overlook could have huge repercussions across your entire project. And the easiest way to make sure that this doesn't happen is make sure that you set your scene scale to Cinometers that way you have a one-to-one match between your digital content creation program and Unreal Engine 5. The next part of our content pipeline, though that I'd like to touch on, deals with textures. Now Unreal behind the scenes has a very, very powerful texture streaming software system that allows textures that are closest to the camera to be shown in full detail and as textures get further and further away, the textures will get lower and lower in detail. The streaming system only works if the textures meet a few requirements. One of those requirements is that the texture needs to be a power of two. So that could be anything from two, four, eight, all the way up to 1024 and 2048, 4096, and 81, 92. So basically anything from two all the way up to eight, hey. Now textures do not need to be square so they don't have to be 64 by 64 or 1024 by 1024. We can have non-square textures so we could have something like 128 by 1024 or 4096 by 16. They just need to have that power of two in either the X or the Y axis. Unreal also supports many different type of source textures including BMP, TARGA, PSD, DNG, JPEG, VDS, and HDR.
