This is course 7, where we will prepare our device to map the real world. We are approaching a new level of reality by fully merging our virtual project with our real world. HoloLens 2 can scan our actual environment and use the data to create a mesh that we can interact with as if it were brought into Unreal Engine like any other mesh. Using the tools we've created and the mechanics we have built throughout this learning path, we are ready to spawn in additional actors to detect and interact with this new environment. Being able to scan our environment to create a mesh that can contain both collision and navigation data for any AI in the scene, we can now adapt to the world directly around us to place our actors so they too can interact with it. This exchange of realities allows an even fuller immersion for the end user. Using our objects onto surfaces using depth cues from our real world and including them when they are behind real world objects will greatly assist in the believability that the holograms are actually in our world. There is a different feeling that comes across when seeing or interacting with a grounded hologram that is occluded by other things around us that makes it so much more real. Using holograms around us also have their advantages as they are there in front of us when we need them and fantasy like when floating around us. Both methods of merging holograms into our world have uses and it is the dynamic experience that we create that will enable the immersion of our users to really benefit from this technology. After preparing our project to interact with our scan spatial data, we will use what we have learned with UI interaction and set up a new system to begin and pause scanning to limit the high performance cost. We'll then demonstrate a system that was built to handle and maintain new and previously created persistent local anchors that we can use to place objects at a consistent spot in our world. We'll then discuss the various scales of mixed reality experiences and end this course by using local anchors in our scanned environment. We'll be able to use tool tips to help us find a switch that needs to be turned on. Because we don't know where it is, we'll need to rely on a loaded anger to assist us. Note that this course covers persistent local anchors and not age your spatial anchors. Those are covered in a later course and explained there.
