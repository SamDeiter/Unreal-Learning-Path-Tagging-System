And so that means that we will use all the distance from the center of the volume all the way up to the edge of our bounding box. Now we do have a problem here in that our spawn area is controlled via a radius setting. We can think of a circle here and we have a center point and then we draw a vector outward toward the edge. This is the radius. This means that the volume of our box is built the same way and so consists of positive and negative numbers either side of the center point. So keeping in mind that the box is being created from the center outwards means that positive numbers would only fill half of our spawn area because the other half actually resides on the negative side of the center. Because of this what we really need to do is control the inverse of the radius amount but as we don't know what radius setting the user will eventually select we really need to set up an automated approach. Let's drag out from the radius variable then and search for and add a multiply maths node. Into its second value we will want to type minus one meaning that the result produced will be the same number as the radius variable only in the negative which is exactly what we need. Let's then pipe the output of that into the min input of our random float in range. We will need two of these as we obviously have two axes which if we don't cater for will essentially give us a random distribution of meshes in a straight line.
