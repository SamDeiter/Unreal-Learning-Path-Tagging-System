In the last few videos we've looked at a variety of ways to control the volume of music layers in response to game events and variables. The use of synchronized layers of music playing in parallel allows us to respond quickly to the game without breaking the underlying musical structures. When using game variables to control music it's always important to think about the player experience from the player's narrative point of view rather than from a literal point of view. For example if you had layers of music associated with the number of enemies in a battle then from a literal point of view it would make sense to have the level of these layers tied to the number of enemies. More enemies equals more intense music. But the outcome of this would be that as you progress through the battle the music would become less and less intense as there was less and less enemies and this might feel anticlimactic. When the player's narrative it's worth considering whether the music should actually become more intense during the final enemy since for the player this is the climax of the battle and this is when they finally win.
