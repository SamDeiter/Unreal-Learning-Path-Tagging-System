Having taken a look at the Skylight's ability to quickly add extra illumination into our level, it is only fair at this point that we also give it Shadow setting some attention. Although, in truth, we don't have an awful lighting wave control on the Skylight itself. As most of the controls for occlusion shadows have found inside the Lightmass section of the world settings tab. When using the Skylight set to static mobility as we are then, our Ambient Occlusion shadows are baked into the Lightmass. But if we switch over to the stationary setting, we gain access to what is known as Bent Normal Occlusion, which can help improve the quality of Ambient Occlusion inside cracks and tight spaces by quite a bit. Let's focus our camera on the bookshelf under the stairs near the computer objects then, where we can see what our light bake look like whilst using the static mobility setting. If we change over to stationary though, and then without changing any other settings at all, we bake our lighting. You can see that we do indeed get a different result, simply by changing the mobility setting on the Skylight. Do keep in mind though that changing the mobility setting, as discussed in a previous video, will also change which of the lights are included in the Skylight calculation at bake time. There will be times of course where all we may want to do is simply add Ambient Light to a scene without adding any occlusion shadows at all. Now to do this, we can simply uncheck the Cast Shadow option and things will now work like an old style Ambient Light with no occlusion shadow information being generated. Since field shadows, although not controlled on the Skylight, can be generated from it when we have moving objects in the scene and so would like to have occlusion shadows be generated by them. Now these are enabled on a per object basis inside the static mesh's lighting tab. Although not much control over shadows can be found on the Skylight actor itself, it is good to know that we can make some changes to the end result by using the mobility setting and by turning occlusion shadows off altogether, should our visualization car for it.
