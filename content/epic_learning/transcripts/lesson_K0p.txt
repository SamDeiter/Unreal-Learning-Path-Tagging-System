Rapping up, we'll now summarize the topics covered in the course and offer some final tips and tricks when working with Magic Leap and Unreal Engine. In this course, we set up our Magic Leap development environment for Unreal Engine. We brought in a Revit model using Datasmith, then located that model with our Touchpad as a controller attachment, and with an image marker. We improved the sense of presence afforded by our model with mesh occlusion and a frame vignette. And in our final module, we got our first glimpse at using hand tracking by toggling between our design options with a left and right hand gesture. Going forward, I encourage you to take what was learned from this foundational course, then mix and match that knowledge for future projects. For example, we've looked at many single interactions paired with a single input method, such as moving an actor by swiping left or toggling a design option with a hand gesture. A good next step might be something relatively simple, like taking the hand gesture method used for toggling design options, and instead allowing it to change the scale of the model, or instead of toggling design options, try toggling different materials. Perhaps you'd like to pair the bumper or the home buttons on the controller with new actions, or use them in lieu of the ones we've already established. By the way, the bumper is motion controller shoulder, and the home button is ML Home Tap. Experiment with lots of input methods to find what feels most natural for your project, or set a redundancies to deal with multiple client preferences. Try using the motion controller with one of its buttons to flip flop between locating two different cubes, then checking the distance between those cubes as a sort of digital tape measure. Did you know that in addition to the actual controller, any of your finger tracking points can also be mapped to a motion controller component? Try having your model follow your right index finger, or attach colliders to your fingers and use trigger boxes to create a control panel full of virtual interactive buttons. Did you know that the Revit model in this project is also featured in a separate course focused on the ArcViz workflow of Revit to Unreal Engine? Try taking the learnings of that course, and the much nicer materials, and applying them to this one. The list goes on and on, and everything mentioned here is also included in the course PDF.
