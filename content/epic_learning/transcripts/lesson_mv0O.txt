Hello and welcome to our Unreal Engine Lighting Basics course. My name is Joe Badley and I am really looking forward to taking a look at the lighting tools and techniques that we are going to be considering here. Inside the class we will take a look first of all at lighting a scene using both point and spotlights taking time to discuss the ways in which we can speed up light placement as we go. Volumes that can help improve the look of our bait lighting as well as the way in which we perceive our lighting will also be covered along with a look at some of the more important global lighting settings. Moving on we will take a look at the light mobility settings and the effect they can have on our visualizations. We will look at controlling and adjusting shadows in the scene as well as the use of IES profiles and powerful light functions that can help change the distribution of light from our illumination points. The light that can help us add natural lighting to a scene will be discussed as well as a consideration of how we can create performance friendly shadows on large outdoor scenes. Adding ambient lighting through the skylight controls will be featured as will use of high dynamic range lighting and atmospheric effects that can help us get better quality from the final piece. Exposure, look up tables, bloom and depth of field are all effects and tools that can change our perception of lighting in a scene and so we will most definitely be looking at those. And then finally we will look at ways of boosting lighting productivity by setting up light actors with their very own construction scripts as well as taking a look at a pre-made fully dynamic day lighting system that is already set up inside of Unreal For us. Without any more preamble from me then let's dive into the Unreal Engine and get to lighting.
