We started this course with probably not the most exciting topic in the world, how to keep your files and assets organized through using naming conventions. Hopefully, as you've been working through the material, you've seen how this can be useful, not only from an organisational standpoint, but also for auditioning and soloing particular sounds or sound events within the editor. We looked at how area loops can bring a subtle background context and atmosphere to your level and how you can then go on to using source loops and one shots to bring the world to life. Using a procedural approach to sound design, where you think about the elements that make up a sound and then sequence or layer these in different ways at runtime, is crucial to creating variety and avoiding repetition. We've covered some of the basic techniques for this and we're sure you'll be able to come up with many more with a bit of experimentation. After dealing with ambient sound actors and sound cues, we've jumped straight into blueprints. Although these may seem a bit intimidating at first, they can be really powerful and a key to getting the most out of Unreal Engine. We've seen already how we can save a lot of work when developing the ambience for your games by creating a blueprint that randomly plays sounds around the player, instead of having to add lots of ambient sound actors by hand. You've probably already got some ideas about how you can develop some of your own custom blueprints, specific to the needs of your game, and through the next few courses you'll learn lots of additional tips, tricks and techniques. If you're not working with a team right now, then a good next step would be to download some of the game levels from the Epic Games Marketplace and populate them with sound. This is a great way to practice and they can also serve as useful portfolio pieces to demonstrate your skills. By bringing together everything you've learned on the course, you should be able to create an immersive and engaging ambient backdrop to your game levels. However, that's all it is so far, a backdrop. We've not really actually interacted much with the audio. This is where things get really interesting, and this will be the topic of the next course.
