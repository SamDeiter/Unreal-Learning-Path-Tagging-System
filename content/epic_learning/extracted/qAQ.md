## Scouting Overview

Scouting is the process of identifying and verifying a location prior to scanning. It is a crucial part of the pre-production for all scans and is essential for successful scanning trips. Scanning a biome may take weeks or more with a full team to capture. Before committing resources, personnel, and time, it is necessary to identify the ideal, subject-rich location to visit. This process includes not only subject quantity and viability, but also determining subject accessibility (permits, tickets, permissions), housing and accommodations for the scanning team, and determining the equipment to bring.

The key tasks of scouting for photogrammetry are:

- Site Selection – First, determine what are the project goals. For example, the project goals may be to digitally recreate seaside cliffs, a medieval castle, or a freeway underpass. Consider which locations likely contain most or all the subjects necessary to complete the project goals.

- Virtual Scouting – Once a location is decided,  online research is performed for the location to ensure it meets expectations. Trip-planning begins at this stage (accommodations, licensing, and permissions, etc.).

- Onsite Scouting – With the location scouted virtually, it is time to verify the location and gather reference images and videos of the actual subjects.

- Reference Photos - Capturing reference data, mostly images and videos of a target location. This process allows the team to review a target location for subject availability and helps in determining what equipment is necessary and how many scan artists will be required.
