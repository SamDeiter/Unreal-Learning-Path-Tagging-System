- [{'type': 'paragraph', 'content': 'Spinning in eddies/vortices : When there are eddies present in the flow, objects can spin in them since rotational components to the force are applied on the exterior surface.'}]
- [{'type': 'paragraph', 'content': 'Drag Model : The force applied to objects is proportional to the surface area exposed to the flow.\xa0 Thus if you push a thin narrow object it’ll travel farther through the water than a wide one.\xa0 Think of aerodynamic kayaks vs a wide raft.'}]
- [{'type': 'paragraph', 'content': 'Waterwheels : Much like the response to eddies, if the edge of a body is exposed to the flow, it can apply a rotational force, and if constraints are setup correctly, you can make waterwheels that spin objects.'}]
- [{'type': 'paragraph', 'content': 'Convex hulls : Convex hulls are used by default, which add additional realism at the cost of some additional simulation sampling.'}]
