## Expedition Planning

This course, Expedition Planning, discusses preparing the scan team for success. This is the most important stage of the entire process because so many things can go wrong on any scan trip. If the scan team cannot scan due to the unforeseen, then there are no scans or digital assets. Expedition planning is a combination of scouting, experience, and  planning.

## Expedition Planning

Through the process of scouting (virtually or on site), and with an ideal location for scanning chosen, it is now time to plan the scanning trip out. How many scan artists are necessary? What equipment is required? Do we have access and permission? These are the things which should be solved prior to the trip. This course covers the critical which need to be considered: Permits and customs, travel and accommodations, and gear and equipment.


### Permits

### Location Permits

Always check for general location permits. Apply for them ahead of the trip. Sometimes there is more than one permit type to apply for. When scouting a location, evaluate everything: transportation, scanning time, how many scan artists would be needed, what exactly the team will scan, what scanning method(s) to use, and the type of equipment to bring.

Some scan methods require additional (or special) permits - Like scanning with a drone for example. Check out the laws for each country and each specific local area. There might be protected areas and wildlife that could be disturbed when it comes to drones.

Additionally, when scanning in natural environments or forests it is wise to check the regional hunting seasons and the general wildlife patterns. Wildlife may alter their expected behavior during particular seasons. Its wise to prepare the scan team with the right kind of aid or protection in case of emergencies.


### Asset Copyright

Sometimes a subject (or asset) is copyrighted and requires additional permissions. Typically this is handled by the legal department, but as a scouting practice, it is ideal to check for any restrictions on creative works. If unsure if something is under copyright or not, and if there isn't a legal team available, then it is best to skip the subject and scan something else instead.

A good guideline is that if the subject is made by nature, it is generally free of copyright. If a subject is manmade, it is best to check for copyright and gain permission/permit for it or do not scan it at all.


### Piloting Drones

When it comes to drone permits, check the local rules and regulations at the scan location. There are different laws regarding drone operation in different countries, and different rules for certain areas in different countries also. There are also different rules regarding flying in public when it comes to distances from people and buildings, or for protected wildlife areas etc. In Sweden, for example, a permit is required from the building owner if a drone is flown within a certain distance from it. Check the local regulations as each region may have unique rules or variations which differ from what you may be familiar with.

Again in Sweden, there is a convenient website and several helpful apps for checking rules regarding drone operation that are updated regularly. The rules around drone piloting are fluid and always changing based on numerous factors (like mating seasons for birds or military practices).

The following link is a useful example of published local regulations for drone pilots:

https://notaminfo.com/swedenmap


### Customs

Check local laws and regulations. Verify that the equipment you are bringing can clear customs (e.g. batteries for airlines). Additionally, it is important to provide a complete equipment list when traveling across national borders with large amounts of equipment.

Ensure that every team member has a valid and up-to-date travel visa/documents. It is not uncommon to have a member believe their documents are valid, only to find out they have expired a year ago (or worse, they will expire mid-trip).

Hotels are typically located in cities and not easy to find near remote scan locations. This alone could turn an ideal scan location into an impractical one because of the daily driving time from hotel to location. Remember, whatever time it takes to get from the hotel to the location is about the same amount of time it takes to get from the location back to the hotel each night.

Another issue with hotels is that the rooms are designed to accommodate visitors with a suitcase or two. The room size is quickly consumed with all of the necessary scan equipment. You can't leave this out in the car in an unfamiliar area and hope its where you left it by morning. Plan with this mindset. So, unless the scan artists want to sleep in a bed with a vegetation scanner next to them, try booking accommodations that have additional space. Like an AirBnB. Hotels are are a good choice for scouting trips when not very heavy on equipment.

With AirBnbs, these accommodations are usually found near any place a tourist may travel. Typically, AirBnBs can be found much closer to the scanning location and often have some kind of garage or patio with space to set up stationary equipment. This allows for bringing plants and vegetation back at the end of the day for scanning in more controlled environments.

The problem with AirBnB sometimes is the space and number of beds. With a large team, it may be necessary to book into several close-by accommodations that are available at the same time. Book early!


### Travel

Scout any area prior to the scan trip. This is more important than it may at first seem. It all traces back to scouting really. The scouting of an area provides a lot of important details about the location and its environment. Ever have a scan artist eaten by a bear? Neither have we! Knowing of that particular kind of wildlife danger ahead of time is essential. How about the number of assets which need to be scanned? How many scan artists will be necessary to scan it all while ensuring full coverage? These are the critical bits of information gathered when scouting a location, which allow for planning what gear or equipment is necessary, clothing to pack, and supplies to bring.

A few additional things to consider/check:

- Check local laws and regulations regarding driving and licenses - including piloting drones.

### Clothing

Wear clothes that are durable. When scanning, it is usually necessary to crawl around on the ground and floor a little to get sufficient coverage of the subject. Pants with support for the knees are great for this, and after a full day of scanning, such pants could be considered essential.

When it comes to scanning outdoors, warm or cold weather, choose clothes using the Three-Layer System: https://www.salomon.com/en-us/running/trail-running-advice/how-properly-dress-layers-the-three-layer-system. Essentially, the Three-Layer System helps you choose the right clothes while taking into account the weather conditions and the activity level, including a base layer for removing moisture, a mid-layer for warmth, and an outer layer to protecting from the elements.

All Weather - Wear reflective vests to be seen by team members more easily in the field and so that other people see you as well. The reflection is great if you get lost or if there are hunters out and about. Bring walkie talkies for easy communication in distant locations where there may be bad phone reception.

Warm Weather - When scanning in warm and sunny weather, the above system is great to have in mind, but remove layers as necessary for comfort. Keep in mind that it is still recommend even in hot outdoor environments to wear long sleeves and pants to protect your skin from the sun, scratches and insects. One of the most important things to remember in warm and sunny weather is wear a hat of some sort. Covering your head, no matter the weather conditions, helps a lot. Wear comfortable, breathable shoes and somewhat water resistant. Wear gloves that allow you to navigate touch screens.

Cold Weather - For cold weather it can be a good idea to bring one large really warm jacket and leave it in the car or at base camp. For sanity and stamina, having the ability to lessen the strain of the elements is often just enough to keep going after a short break. Bring a pair of thin gloves with fingers and a pair of really warm gloves that can be worn on lunch breaks or when the cold gets unbearable.


### Gear

Knowing what to bring is as important as bringing it. You could just bring it all, but after doing that once, quickly the benefit of traveling as light as possible becomes obvious. Plan out the necessary gear for each scan trip, bringing the gear necessary to capture the target subjects according to the project goals.
